# DBA 5Ps Exam Study Guide

Course: [[Digital Business Analysis (ISYS90049)/Course Overview|Digital Business Analysis]]  
Framework: **Plan -> Problem -> Prize -> Propose -> Prove**  
Use: organise DBA exam study around the way a business analyst thinks through a digital change problem.

## Core Memory Line

> A BA **Plans** the analysis, understands the **Problem**, defines the **Prize**, **Proposes** a suitable solution, and **Proves** whether it delivered value.

This does not replace the official exam sections. It gives a simpler mental map for answering them.

| 5P phase | Plain meaning | Official exam section link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | How will the BA work be done? | Section 1: Planning |
| Problem | What is really happening and why? | Section 1 and Section 2 |
| Prize | What should success look like? | Section 2 and Section 3 |
| Propose | What solution/change path best fits? | Section 2 and Section 3 |
| Prove | Did the solution deliver value? | Section 4: Solution Evaluation |

## What the 5Ps Fix

The mock exams and guided practice repeatedly test the same risk: a manager wants to jump straight to a digital solution. The 5Ps stop that:

1. Do not recommend a system before planning the analysis.
2. Do not define requirements before understanding the current state and root causes.
3. Do not choose a solution before defining future-state success.
4. Do not propose a solution without comparing value, feasibility, risk, and readiness.
5. Do not call a solution successful without measuring business value.

## Phase 1 - Plan

### What This Phase Means

Plan is about setting up the business analysis work. The BA decides how they will investigate the need, who they need to involve, what evidence they need, how decisions will be made, and what risks might weaken the analysis.

### What to Study

| Area | Must know |
| --- | --- |
| BA planning tasks | Plan BA approach, stakeholder engagement, governance, information management, and BA performance improvement. |
| Planning aspects | Background, context, scope, approach, activities, complexity, risk, approval, time, cost, resources. |
| Governance | Decision-making process, change control process, approvals, prioritisation approach. |
| Information management | How BA information is documented, stored, shared, accessed, retained, and protected. |
| BA risks | Time pressure, stakeholder access, unclear scope, weak governance, fragmented information, low domain knowledge. |
| Approach | Predictive, adaptive, or hybrid depending on uncertainty, complexity, regulation, and governance. |
| Context setup | Which internal/external context techniques and elicitation activities will be used. |

### Techniques and Outputs

| Technique/task | Output |
| --- | --- |
| BA plan | Planned analysis approach, activities, timing, methods, tools, and deliverables. |
| Stakeholder engagement planning | Who to involve, why, when, how, and for what information. |
| Governance planning | Who approves, who decides, how changes are raised, assessed, and approved. |
| Information management planning | Rules for BA documents, privacy, access, storage, sharing, and reuse. |
| Complexity/risk analysis | BA work risks, consequences, severity, and mitigations. |

### Exam Triggers

| If the question says... | Think Plan |
| --- | --- |
| "Prepare a Business Analysis Plan within one month/two weeks" | Discuss BA planning challenges, scope, risks, stakeholders, governance, and activities. |
| "What challenges could the BA encounter?" | Focus on risks to the BA work, not only solution risks. |
| "What activities should the BA plan to perform?" | Name analysis activities and pair them with elicitation techniques. |
| "Who should approve?" or "who owns decisions?" | Use BA governance and approvals. |
| "How should the BA manage stakeholders?" | Use stakeholder engagement planning, not only stakeholder identification. |

### What a Strong Plan Answer Includes

- the business need or perceived problem;
- the BA approach and why it fits the uncertainty/time pressure;
- stakeholder access and elicitation methods;
- governance, approvals, change control, and prioritisation;
- systems, documents, processes, or data to review;
- risks to the analysis work and mitigations;
- outputs such as BA plan, stakeholder plan, governance approach, or risk log.

### Common Plan Mistakes

- Talking about final implementation risks instead of BA planning risks.
- Listing stakeholders without explaining how they will be engaged.
- Ignoring governance and approvals.
- Saying "interviews and workshops" without explaining why those techniques fit.
- Treating the solution request as already valid.

## Phase 2 - Problem

### What This Phase Means

Problem is where the BA builds context, describes the current state, and diagnoses what is really causing the business issue. This phase prevents the BA from treating symptoms, stakeholder preferences, or competitor-copying as the real need.

### What to Study

| Area | Must know |
| --- | --- |
| Business need | The problem or opportunity explaining why change may be required. |
| Current state | How the organisation currently operates in relation to the need. |
| Context analysis | External and internal factors affecting the problem and possible solution. |
| POPIT | People, organisation, process, information, and technology dimensions. |
| Stakeholders | Who is affected, who has knowledge, who decides, who resists, and who must adopt change. |
| Elicitation | Document analysis, interviews, surveys, observation, workshops, focus groups. |
| Root cause | Difference between symptom, cause, and root cause. |
| Evidence | Triangulate stakeholder views, documents, operational data, and system/process evidence. |

### Techniques and Outputs

| Technique/task | Output |
| --- | --- |
| PEST/PESTLE | External pressures and constraints. |
| Porter's Five Forces | Industry and competitive pressure. |
| SWOT | Internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats. |
| POPIT | Holistic current-state or scope view. |
| Stakeholder analysis | Involvement, attitude, influence, impact, and engagement needs. |
| Document analysis | Existing evidence, conflicts, gaps, policies, reports, and data. |
| Interviews/surveys/workshops/observation | Elicited evidence from stakeholders and work practices. |
| Process modelling | Handoffs, bottlenecks, rework, delays, and process issues. |
| Customer journey map | User/customer touchpoints, emotions, pain points, and opportunities. |
| Five Whys | Narrow causal chain. |
| Fishbone diagram | Multiple possible causes by category. |
| Pareto analysis | Prioritised causes by frequency or impact. |

### Exam Triggers

| If the question says... | Think Problem |
| --- | --- |
| "The manager wants to build an app immediately" | Explain why current state and root need must be analysed first. |
| "Discuss context techniques" | Use PEST, Five Forces, SWOT, stakeholder analysis, POPIT, trend analysis, or journey mapping. |
| "Current state analysis is unnecessary" | Defend current-state analysis using POPIT, business need, scope, evidence, and risk reduction. |
| "Which technique would identify the issue?" | Match technique to issue: process model, Five Whys, fishbone, Pareto, data analysis. |
| "How do you know it is the real cause?" | Use evidence, triangulation, experiments, data validation, and stakeholder confirmation. |

### What a Strong Problem Answer Includes

- visible symptoms from the case;
- possible causes and why they need evidence;
- current-state analysis using POPIT or process/customer journey views;
- stakeholder evidence and elicitation techniques;
- root-cause technique matched to the issue;
- explanation of how the analysis avoids recommending the wrong solution.

### Common Problem Mistakes

- Treating "the system is bad" as a root cause.
- Naming Five Whys, fishbone, or Pareto without explaining the evidence needed.
- Using a customer journey map as an internal process map.
- Describing general company background instead of current-state facts tied to the need.
- Ignoring stakeholder disagreement as useful evidence.

## Phase 3 - Prize

### What This Phase Means

Prize means defining what success should look like before choosing detailed solution design. This phase translates the diagnosed need into a future state, goals, SMART objectives, scope, requirements, and measures of value.

### What to Study

| Area | Must know |
| --- | --- |
| Future state | Desired business conditions after change. |
| Business need -> requirement -> goal -> objective | The chain from why change is needed to measurable success. |
| SMART objectives | Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bounded targets. |
| Solution scope | What must change to reach the future state. |
| Constraints | Budget, time, technology, resource, policy, and regulation limits. |
| Requirement types | Business, stakeholder, solution, functional, non-functional, and transition requirements. |
| Requirement quality | Atomic, complete, consistent, concise, feasible, unambiguous, testable, prioritised, understandable. |
| Verification vs validation | Written well versus right for the business need. |
| Success measures | Measures, metrics, and KPIs that will later prove value. |

### Techniques and Outputs

| Technique/task | Output |
| --- | --- |
| Future state analysis | Desired outcomes, value, conditions, and capabilities. |
| SMART objective writing | Measurable success targets. |
| Solution scope definition | Boundary of what needs to change. |
| Requirements classification | Clear requirement categories. |
| Requirements traceability | Links from need to requirements and design/evaluation. |
| Verification | Requirement quality check. |
| Validation | Alignment check against need, goals, scope, and stakeholders. |
| KPI design | Strategic measures of intended value. |

### Exam Triggers

| If the question says... | Think Prize |
| --- | --- |
| "Define business need and business requirement" | Explain why change is needed and what high-level outcome is required. |
| "Future state is unnecessary" | Explain that future state defines success, value, scope, gaps, and change strategy. |
| "Write functional and non-functional requirements" | Classify requirements and justify why each matters. |
| "Verify requirements" | Use good requirement attributes. |
| "Requirements are verified but not right" | Explain validation: they may not align with business need or goals. |
| "Define KPIs" | Link KPIs to goals/objectives, not to available data only. |

### What a Strong Prize Answer Includes

- business need and business requirement distinction;
- future state described as desired "what", not vendor-specific "how";
- goals and SMART objectives;
- solution scope and constraints;
- requirement categories and quality attributes;
- success measures or KPIs that connect to business objectives.

### Common Prize Mistakes

- Treating "launch the system by August" as enough of a business objective.
- Making the future state too detailed or vendor-specific.
- Confusing solution scope with the final solution.
- Writing vague non-functional requirements such as "fast" or "easy to use".
- Confusing SMART objectives with system requirements.
- Treating verification and validation as the same.

## Phase 4 - Propose

### What This Phase Means

Propose is where the BA uses the problem diagnosis and the prize definition to compare solution options and recommend a change approach. The BA does not simply pick the first technology idea. They compare value, cost, feasibility, risk, readiness, constraints, and requirement fit.

### What to Study

| Area | Must know |
| --- | --- |
| Gap analysis | Difference between current and future states, with actions to close gaps. |
| Change strategy | High-level approach for moving from current state to future state. |
| Enterprise readiness | Whether people, processes, structure, training, support, and culture can sustain change. |
| Risk assessment | Likelihood, impact, timing, warning signs, tolerance, and treatment. |
| Implementation approaches | Single-stage, gradual/phased, incremental, and iterative. |
| Design options | Buy, build, configure, combine, redesign process, change policy, change organisation, or improve data/integration. |
| Requirements architecture | How requirements, views, dependencies, conflicts, and models fit together. |
| Modelling/design tools | BPMN/process models, matrices, wireframes, prototypes, use cases, data/interface/state models. |
| Value assessment | Expected benefits, costs, feasibility, risk, and alignment. |

### Techniques and Outputs

| Technique/task | Output |
| --- | --- |
| Gap analysis | Gaps, responsible units, actions, examples, and effort estimate. |
| Readiness assessment | Adoption risks, training needs, support needs, resistance, and capacity issues. |
| Risk register | Risks, likelihood, impact, treatment, warning signs. |
| Change strategy | Implementation approach and transition rationale. |
| Design options | Viable options compared against requirements and value. |
| Wireframes/prototypes | Early representation of interface or behaviour. |
| Use cases | Actor-system interaction, normal flow, alternative flow, include/extend logic. |
| Potential value analysis | Recommendation based on benefits, costs, feasibility, risk, and fit. |

### Exam Triggers

| If the question says... | Think Propose |
| --- | --- |
| "How should the BA prevent interruption during implementation?" | Use phased, pilot, low-risk periods, contingency, or temporary support. |
| "Compare design options" | Use value, cost, risk, feasibility, requirements, future state, and readiness. |
| "Which representation for IT?" | Use BPMN, UML/use case, matrices, data/interface models depending on audience and information. |
| "Write a use case narrative" | Use actors, trigger, pre-condition, normal flow, alternative flow, post-condition, include, extend. |
| "A stakeholder requests a feature outside scope" | Assess requirement change against scope, value, risk, time, resources, and governance. |
| "High value but high adoption risk" | Present trade-off and readiness actions, not just benefits. |

### What a Strong Propose Answer Includes

- gap between current and future state;
- options, not only one preferred system;
- requirement fit and traceability;
- value, cost, feasibility, and risk comparison;
- readiness and transition considerations;
- recommendation framed as analyst advice, with authorised stakeholders deciding.

### Common Propose Mistakes

- Recommending a tool without comparing alternatives.
- Ignoring adoption, training, support, and readiness.
- Treating use cases and wireframes as interchangeable.
- Providing design detail without linking it back to requirements.
- Forgetting transition requirements.
- Overstating benefits without evidence.

## Phase 5 - Prove

### What This Phase Means

Prove is solution evaluation. The BA checks whether the implemented or active solution delivers the intended business value. This is not just "was the project delivered?" It asks whether the solution actually improved the business situation.

### What to Study

| Area | Must know |
| --- | --- |
| Solution evaluation tasks | Measure performance, analyse measures, assess solution limitations, assess enterprise limitations, recommend actions. |
| Measures, metrics, KPIs | Raw values, derived/interpreted values, and key metrics tied to objectives. |
| Good metrics | Clear, relevant, economical, adequate, quantifiable, trustworthy, credible. |
| Continuous monitoring | Intervals, trends, delayed benefits, and response actions. |
| Solution limitations | Internal solution issues such as missing features, poor usability, weak integration, bad data, slow performance. |
| Enterprise limitations | Organisational issues such as training, resistance, process mismatch, policy conflict, low readiness. |
| Benchmarking | Internal, competitive, collaborative, shadow, functional, or world-class comparisons. |
| A/B testing | Comparing solution variants against a chosen metric. |

### Techniques and Outputs

| Technique/task | Output |
| --- | --- |
| Measure solution performance | Performance data. |
| Analyse performance measures | Variance between expected and actual value. |
| Limitation assessment | Solution and enterprise barriers to value. |
| KPI monitoring | Evidence of progress against objectives. |
| Benchmarking | Comparison to internal or external performance standards. |
| A/B testing | Evidence on which variant performs better. |
| Recommendation to increase value | Improve, adjust, train, change process, add feature, retire, or do nothing for now. |

### Exam Triggers

| If the question says... | Think Prove |
| --- | --- |
| "Define a measure, metrics, and KPIs" | Use the measure -> metric -> KPI distinction and link KPIs to objectives. |
| "Why choose these KPIs?" | Explain how each KPI shows intended value. |
| "How often should KPIs be measured?" | Choose interval based on business cycle, adoption, and usefulness. |
| "The solution works but value is missing" | Assess solution and enterprise limitations. |
| "Processing time improved but complaints increased" | Analyse variance and avoid relying on one metric. |
| "Benchmark solution performance" | Choose the benchmarking type and KPI that fits the case. |

### What a Strong Prove Answer Includes

- intended objective or value;
- relevant measure, metric, and KPI distinction;
- data source and measurement frequency;
- comparison between expected and actual performance;
- possible solution and enterprise limitations;
- recommendation that addresses the cause of the value gap.

### Common Prove Mistakes

- Calling every metric a KPI.
- Choosing metrics because data is easy to collect.
- Measuring activity instead of business value.
- Ignoring adoption delay or sample size.
- Assuming a technical change is needed when training, process, or policy is the limitation.
- Forgetting benchmarking frequency and comparison source.

## How to Study With the 5Ps

### Step 1 - Memorise the Skeleton

Write this from memory until it is automatic:

```text
Plan: BA approach, stakeholders, governance, information, risks.
Problem: context, current state, evidence, stakeholders, root cause.
Prize: future state, goals, SMART objectives, scope, requirements, KPIs.
Propose: gaps, readiness, design options, change strategy, recommendation.
Prove: measures, metrics, KPIs, limitations, benchmarking, value improvement.
```

### Step 2 - Attach Techniques to Each P

| Phase | Technique cluster |
| --- | --- |
| Plan | BA plan, stakeholder engagement plan, governance, approvals, change control, information management, BA risk assessment. |
| Problem | PEST, Five Forces, SWOT, POPIT, stakeholder analysis, elicitation, current state, journey maps, process models, Five Whys, fishbone, Pareto. |
| Prize | Future state, SMART objectives, solution scope, requirement classification, verification, validation, traceability, KPI design. |
| Propose | Gap analysis, readiness assessment, risk register, transition planning, design options, wireframes, prototypes, use cases, value comparison. |
| Prove | Measure/metric/KPI, performance analysis, solution limitations, enterprise limitations, benchmarking, A/B testing, continuous monitoring. |

### Step 3 - Practise Trigger Recognition

For each practice question, first label it with one or more Ps:

| Question type | Likely P |
| --- | --- |
| BA plan, planning challenges, risks, governance | Plan |
| Context techniques, current state, stakeholder evidence | Problem |
| Root cause, symptoms, process issue, technique choice | Problem |
| Future state, SMART objective, requirement types | Prize |
| Functional/non-functional, verification/validation, use case | Prize and Propose |
| Gap analysis, readiness, implementation approach, design options | Propose |
| Metrics, KPIs, benchmarking, solution/enterprise limitations | Prove |

### Step 4 - Use the 6-Sentence Answer Drill

For a scenario question, write one sentence for each step:

1. **Need:** The business need is...
2. **Plan:** The BA should plan...
3. **Problem:** The BA should analyse the current state/root cause by...
4. **Prize:** Success should be defined through...
5. **Propose:** The BA should compare options using...
6. **Prove:** The solution should be evaluated by...

Then expand whichever phase the question is actually asking about.

### Step 5 - Mark Yourself Against Exam Expectations

After each answer, check:

- Did I use case facts?
- Did I name the technique or concept?
- Did I define it briefly?
- Did I explain why it fits?
- Did I identify an output?
- Did I link the output to value, risk, stakeholder alignment, decision quality, or requirements quality?
- Did I avoid jumping to a solution?

> **Plan** the work.  
> Diagnose the **Problem**.  
> Define the **Prize**.  
> **Propose** the change.  
> **Prove** the value.
