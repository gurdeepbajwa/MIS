# DBA Flashcard Deck

Course: [[Digital Business Analysis (ISYS90049)/Course Overview|Digital Business Analysis]]
Purpose: active recall for the DBA exam.
Sources: [[DBA Exam Cheat Sheet]], [[DBA Techniques Cheat Sheet]], [[DBA 5Ps Exam Study Guide]], [[DBA Weak Areas Cheat Sheet]]

## How to Use

Cover the `Back` and `Exam use` columns before answering. Say the answer aloud in 20-40 seconds, then check whether you named the formal DBA concept, tied it to the case, identified the output, and linked it to value, risk, stakeholder alignment, decision quality, or requirements quality.

Use this marking code while studying:

- `Hard`: could not recall the concept or distinction.
- `Partial`: remembered the idea but missed the formal category, output, or exam use.
- `Ready`: can answer clearly and apply it to a scenario.

## Core Spine Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-01 | What are the 5Ps memory spine for DBA? | Plan, Problem, Prize, Propose, Prove. | Use these to stop yourself jumping straight to a digital solution. |
| DBA-02 | What does `Plan` mean? | Set up the BA work: approach, stakeholders, elicitation, governance, information management, scope, risks, and deliverables. | Use for BA plan, planning challenges, governance, approvals, and stakeholder-engagement questions. |
| DBA-03 | What does `Problem` mean? | Understand context, current state, evidence, stakeholders, symptoms, causes, and root causes before accepting a solution. | Use for context, current-state, elicitation, root-cause, and technique-choice questions. |
| DBA-04 | What does `Prize` mean? | Define future-state success before detailed design: goals, SMART objectives, solution scope, constraints, requirements, and KPIs. | Use for need, requirement, objective, future state, verification, validation, and KPI questions. |
| DBA-05 | What does `Propose` mean? | Compare solution and change options using gaps, readiness, requirements fit, value, cost, risk, feasibility, and transition. | Use for gap analysis, implementation approach, design options, use cases, and change strategy. |
| DBA-06 | What does `Prove` mean? | Evaluate whether the implemented or active solution delivers intended business value. | Use for measures, metrics, KPIs, limitations, benchmarking, A/B testing, and value-improvement questions. |
| DBA-07 | What is the DBA core answer formula? | Identify the business need, name the concept/technique, define it briefly, apply case facts, state the output, and explain value, decision quality, stakeholder alignment, or risk reduction. | Use for almost every written answer. |
| DBA-08 | What is the 6-sentence DBA drill? | Need; Plan; Problem; Prize; Propose; Prove. Expand the phase the question asks about. | Use when a scenario is broad and you need a quick answer structure. |
| DBA-09 | What is the main DBA exam danger? | Recommending a technology before planning the analysis, understanding the problem, defining success, comparing options, or proving value. | Use as a guardrail in every scenario. |
| DBA-10 | What formal-category check should you do before answering? | Decide whether the question asks for a context technique, BA activity, or elicitation technique. | This fixes the common weak-area pattern of giving a sensible but wrong category. |

## Planning Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |

| DBA-11 | What are the five BA planning tasks? | Plan BA approach, plan stakeholder engagement, plan BA governance, plan BA information management, and identify BA performance improvements. | Use for planning-section recall. |

| DBA-13 | BA work risk vs solution risk? | BA work risks weaken the analysis process; solution risks affect the final change or implemented solution. | Use when the question asks for planning challenges. |
| DBA-14 | What are common BA planning risks? | Poor stakeholder access, unclear scope, time pressure, weak governance, fragmented information, and low domain knowledge. | Pair each risk with a consequence and mitigation. |
| DBA-15 | What is BA governance planning? | Clarifying decision rights, approvals, prioritisation, change control, escalation, and who has authority to approve outcomes. | Use when the scenario has slow, contested, or unclear decisions. |

| DBA-17 | Predictive vs adaptive vs hybrid BA approach? | Predictive suits clear scope and direction; adaptive suits uncertainty and feedback; hybrid combines formal governance with iterative discovery or delivery. | Explain which parts are formal and which parts iterate. |

## Context, Scope, And Elicitation Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-19 | PEST/PESTLE: what is it best for? | External political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental influences. | Use when the case needs broad external risk or opportunity scanning. |
| DBA-20 | Porter's Five Forces: what is it best for? | Industry and competitive pressure. | Do not use it for internal process analysis. |
| DBA-21 | SWOT: what is it best for? | Combining internal strengths/weaknesses with external opportunities/threats. | Keep internal and external factors separate. |
| DBA-22 | POPIT: what is it best for? | Analysing people, organisation, process, information, and technology together. | Use when the case is broader than technology. |
| DBA-23 | What are the POPIT dimensions? | People, Organisation, Process, Information, Technology. | Use as a current-state, scope, or future-state checklist. |
| DBA-24 | Influence-impact matrix: what does it produce? | Stakeholder-management categories such as key players, keep satisfied, keep informed, and monitor. | Do not confuse high impact with high influence. |
| DBA-25 | Onion diagram vs influence-impact matrix? | Onion diagram shows closeness to the initiative; influence-impact matrix shows how closely stakeholders should be managed. | Closeness is not authority. |
| DBA-26 | Document analysis: best use and trap? | Reviews existing documents, reports, policies, models, data, or instructions; trap is assuming documents are current, complete, or consistent. | Use as an early evidence-gathering technique. |
| DBA-27 | Interview vs survey vs observation? | Interview gives deep qualitative insight; survey gathers structured input from many people; observation shows work as actually performed. | Match the technique to depth, breadth, or real-practice evidence. |
| DBA-28 | Workshop vs focus group? | Workshop creates shared understanding, models, decisions, or validated outputs; focus group gathers group feedback on needs, issues, concepts, or reactions. | Avoid workshops for sensitive issues too early. |
| DBA-29 | What is triangulation? | Comparing interviews, workshops, documents, observation, and data to validate problem evidence. | One stakeholder view is not enough for a strong root-cause claim. |

## Problem And Strategy Analysis Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-30 | Business need vs business requirement? | Need explains why change is required; business requirement states the high-level outcome or condition needed to address the need. | Use before writing goals, objectives, or solution options. |
| DBA-31 | Need, requirement, solution, constraint: keep them separate. | Need is why; requirement is what must be achieved; solution is how it might be delivered; constraint is a limit such as deadline, budget, policy, or technology. | Treat deadlines as constraints, not reasons to skip analysis. |
| DBA-32 | Business goal vs business objective? | Goal is qualitative; objective is measurable and should be SMART. | Do not write a system feature as a business objective. |
| DBA-33 | What is the strategy-analysis flow? | Business need -> business requirement -> business goal -> SMART objective. | Use this chain to organise answers. |
| DBA-34 | What is current-state analysis? | Focused, evidence-based explanation of how the organisation currently operates in relation to the need. | Do not write general company background. |
| DBA-35 | Symptom vs cause vs root cause? | Symptom is the observable effect; cause explains why it happens; root cause is the deeper cause that prevents recurrence if addressed. | Do not treat a symptom as the problem. |
| DBA-36 | Five Whys: best use and trap? | Probes a narrow causal chain; trap is oversimplifying complex, multi-cause problems. | Needs evidence and the right stakeholders. |
| DBA-37 | Fishbone diagram: best use and trap? | Organises multiple possible causes into categories; trap is that it does not prioritise causes by itself. | Pair with evidence or Pareto when prioritisation matters. |
| DBA-38 | Pareto analysis: best use and trap? | Prioritises causes by frequency, impact, or contribution; trap is needing good data and not confusing frequency with importance. | Add business impact criteria, not only counts. |
| DBA-39 | Process model: what does it reveal? | Workflow, handoffs, decisions, bottlenecks, delays, rework, and process logic. | A process model supports analysis; it is not automatically the solution. |
| DBA-40 | Customer journey map: what does it reveal? | User/customer phases, actions, thoughts, feelings, touchpoints, pain points, and opportunities. | Use the customer's perspective, not internal process steps. |
| DBA-41 | Current state vs future state? | Current state describes what happens now; future state describes desired business conditions after change. | Define the "what" before the detailed "how". |
| DBA-42 | Solution scope vs solution? | Solution scope defines what must change; solution defines how the change will be made. | Do not confuse the boundary of change with the final design. |
| DBA-43 | Gap analysis: what is its output? | Gaps between current and future state, with responsible units, actions, examples, and effort or size estimates. | Gaps can cross POPIT areas. |
| DBA-44 | Readiness assessment: why does it matter? | It checks whether people, processes, structure, training, support, culture, and capacity can adopt and sustain the change. | A good solution can fail if the enterprise is not ready. |

## Requirements And Design Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-45 | What are the requirement types? | Business, stakeholder, solution, functional, non-functional, and transition requirements. | Classify before writing requirements. |
| DBA-46 | Functional vs non-functional requirement? | Functional says what the solution must do; non-functional says how well or under what conditions it must operate. | Convert vague quality words into measurable criteria. |
| DBA-47 | What are transition requirements? | Temporary capabilities or conditions needed to move from current state to future state, such as migration, training, support, or rollout readiness. | Do not forget them in implementation questions. |
| DBA-48 | What are good requirement attributes? | Atomic, complete, consistent, concise, feasible, unambiguous, testable, prioritised, and understandable. | Use for verification questions. |
| DBA-49 | Verification vs validation? | Verification asks whether the requirement is written and modelled well; validation asks whether it is the right requirement for the need, goals, scope, and stakeholders. | A verified requirement can still fail validation. |
| DBA-50 | What is TMPAA? | Trace, Maintain, Prioritise, Assess changes, Approve requirements. | Use for requirements lifecycle management. |
| DBA-51 | What is the traceability chain? | Business need -> business requirement -> stakeholder requirement -> solution requirement -> functional/non-functional/transition requirement. | Use traceability for impact analysis and alignment. |
| DBA-52 | MoSCoW: what does it do? | Classifies requirements as must, should, could, or will not for now. | "Must" should mean genuinely required, not merely desired. |
| DBA-53 | BPMN/process model vs matrix? | BPMN/process model shows activity flow and process logic; a matrix compares structured information such as requirements, stakeholders, priority, value, or traceability. | Choose the representation for the information and audience. |
| DBA-54 | Wireframe vs prototype? | Wireframe is low-fidelity interface structure; prototype represents solution behaviour or design at low, medium, or high fidelity. | Higher fidelity can create false certainty and cost more to change. |
| DBA-55 | Use case: what are its elements? | Actor, trigger, pre-condition, normal flow, alternative flow, and post-condition. | Use for actor-system interaction scenarios. |
| DBA-56 | Include vs extend in use cases? | Include is a required interaction reused by another use case; extend is optional or conditional behaviour added under certain conditions. | Use correctly when describing use-case relationships. |
| DBA-57 | Design options: what should they be compared against? | Requirements, future state, value, cost, feasibility, risk, readiness, constraints, and alignment. | The analyst recommends; authorised stakeholders decide. |

## Change And Implementation Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-58 | Single-stage implementation: when suitable? | Smaller or tightly coupled changes where one major release is practical. | Use this term instead of informal labels. |
| DBA-59 | Gradual/phased implementation: when useful? | Large, risky, or disruptive changes that benefit from stages. | Explain how stages reduce risk or disruption. |
| DBA-60 | Incremental implementation: what is the logic? | Deliver useful capability in increments, with each increment adding value or coverage. | Use when progressive value delivery matters. |
| DBA-61 | Iterative implementation: what is the logic? | Refine the solution through repeated feedback cycles. | Use when uncertainty requires learning and adjustment. |
| DBA-62 | What should risk assessment include? | Likelihood, impact, timing, warning signs, tolerance, and treatment. | Link risks to objectives, transition, or value realisation. |
| DBA-63 | What are risk treatment choices? | Avoid, transfer/share, mitigate, accept, or increase readiness. | Match treatment to the specific risk. |
| DBA-64 | What should a strong Propose answer include? | Gap, options, requirements fit, value, cost, feasibility, risk, readiness, transition, and recommendation. | Do not recommend a tool without comparing alternatives. |

## Solution Evaluation Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-65 | What are the five solution evaluation tasks? | Measure solution performance, analyse performance measures, assess solution limitations, assess enterprise limitations, recommend actions to increase value. | Memorise these exactly for evaluation questions. |
| DBA-66 | What is solution evaluation for? | Checking whether an implemented or active solution delivers intended business value. | It is not only a project delivery review. |
| DBA-67 | Measure vs metric vs KPI? | Measure is a direct count/value; metric is a derived or interpreted value; KPI is a key metric tied to a strategic objective. | Not every metric is a KPI. |
| DBA-68 | What is the measurement chain? | Goals/objectives -> critical success factors -> KPIs -> metrics -> measures. | Use when justifying KPIs. |
| DBA-69 | What makes a good metric? | Clear, relevant, economical, adequate, quantifiable, trustworthy, and credible. | Do not choose metrics only because data is easy to collect. |
| DBA-70 | What is a vanity or activity metric trap? | Choosing measures such as clicks, logins, messages, or comments that do not show business value. | Prefer value indicators like retention, churn, conversion, satisfaction, completion, or average order value when relevant. |
| DBA-71 | Solution limitation vs enterprise limitation? | Solution limitation is inside the solution; enterprise limitation is in the organisation around the solution. | If the system works but value is missing, check training, process, readiness, policy, resistance, and culture. |
| DBA-72 | Benchmarking: what is it? | Comparing performance against internal units, competitors, peers, public data, functional equivalents, or leading performers. | Choose the benchmarking type that fits the question. |
| DBA-73 | Internal vs competitive vs collaborative vs shadow benchmarking? | Internal compares within the organisation; competitive compares competitors; collaborative shares data with peers; shadow uses public competitor or industry data. | For industry comparison, use competitive, collaborative, or shadow benchmarking. |
| DBA-74 | A/B testing: what is it and what is the trap? | Comparing two solution variants against a selected metric; trap is using too little data volume or the wrong metric. | Use for variant-choice evaluation. |
| DBA-75 | What should a strong Prove answer include? | Intended objective, measure/metric/KPI distinction, data source, frequency, expected vs actual performance, solution and enterprise limitations, and value-improvement recommendation. | Do not recommend actions before explaining evidence and limitation. |

## Technique Pairing Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-76 | PEST + SWOT: why pair them? | PEST identifies external forces; SWOT converts internal and external context into strategic implications. | Use for context-to-strategy answers. |
| DBA-77 | POPIT + gap analysis: why pair them? | POPIT structures current/future state; gap analysis identifies required changes across those dimensions. | Use for holistic change answers. |
| DBA-78 | Stakeholder matrix + elicitation plan: why pair them? | The matrix shows who matters; the plan explains how to involve them. | Avoid reducing stakeholder work to a list. |
| DBA-79 | Document analysis + interviews: why pair them? | Documents provide existing evidence; interviews explain context, exceptions, and assumptions. | Use when source evidence may be incomplete or outdated. |
| DBA-80 | Observation + process model: why pair them? | Observation shows real work; the process model makes workflows and bottlenecks visible. | Use when actual practice differs from documented process. |
| DBA-81 | Fishbone + Pareto: why pair them? | Fishbone organises possible causes; Pareto prioritises causes with evidence of impact. | Use for root-cause prioritisation. |
| DBA-82 | Personas + journey maps: why pair them? | Personas define user segments; journey maps show their experience across touchpoints. | Use for customer/user-centred analysis. |
| DBA-83 | SMART objectives + KPIs: why pair them? | Objectives define the target; KPIs monitor whether the target is achieved. | Use when defining future-state success and evaluation. |
| DBA-84 | Use cases + wireframes: why pair them? | Use cases show interaction flow; wireframes show interface structure. | Use for requirements-to-design communication. |
| DBA-85 | Traceability + validation: why pair them? | Traceability links requirements to needs; validation checks whether those links are meaningful. | Use for requirements quality and alignment answers. |

## Last-Minute Check Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DBA-86 | What should you always state before the solution? | The business need. | Prevents solution-first answers. |
| DBA-87 | When should you use POPIT? | When the case is broader than technology and involves people, organisation, process, information, and technology. | Prevents technology-only analysis. |
| DBA-88 | What must you say after naming a technique? | Why it fits the case and what output it produces. | Prevents name-dropping techniques. |
| DBA-89 | What must KPIs link to? | Business goals, objectives, critical success factors, and intended value. | Prevents activity-based KPI answers. |
| DBA-90 | What should you check before submitting a DBA answer? | Formal category, need/requirement/solution/constraint separation, requirement quality, KPI value link, solution-evaluation task, evidence, and value. | Use as the final exam checklist. |
