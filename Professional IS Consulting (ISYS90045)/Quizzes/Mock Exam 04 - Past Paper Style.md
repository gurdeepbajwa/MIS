## Q1. Unstructured Problem Solving

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

SilverTree Insurance is losing small-business customers. The sales director says competitors have cheaper policies. Claims staff say customers are frustrated because claims status is hard to track. The IT director says the ageing policy platform cannot support real-time updates. The CEO believes the answer is to outsource customer support to reduce costs. A board member argues that the company should redesign the customer journey before changing technology or staffing.

The CEO asks your firm: should SilverTree outsource customer support to reduce churn?

(A) Is this a structured or unstructured problem? Why? (1 mark)

(B) What framework would you use to help SilverTree understand and solve the problem? Identify each step and outline how each step would apply. (9 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/Lecture|Week 10 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process|How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process]].

How to answer: identify competing explanations and uncertain criteria. Apply the six-step process and make sure criteria are chosen before alternatives. Possible criteria include churn reduction, customer experience, cost, implementation risk, claims accuracy, staff impact, and time to value.

## Q2. Meetings, Interviews, and Workshops

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are leading discovery for a not-for-profit, CareLink, which wants to improve volunteer onboarding. Volunteers complain about slow approvals. Program managers say training is inconsistent. The safeguarding officer says background checks cannot be rushed. The executive director wants a workshop next week with all staff and volunteers to "sort everything out quickly".

(A) Explain when a consultant should use a meeting, an interview, and a workshop. (3 marks)

(B) Design an initial engagement plan using at least one meeting, one interview activity, and one workshop activity. Explain the purpose of each. (4 marks)

(C) Identify three facilitation or stakeholder risks in the executive director's request for one large workshop, and explain how you would manage them. (3 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 08/Lecture|Week 08 Lecture]] for meetings and interviews, and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 07/Lecture|Week 07 Lecture]] for workshops.

How to answer: choose the format based on evidence need and power dynamics. Sensitive issues such as safeguarding and volunteer experience may need interviews first. A workshop can then validate process maps or prioritise improvements after evidence has been gathered.

## Q3. Engagement Lifecycle and Proposals

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

Your firm is invited to respond to a request from UrbanGrid, a utilities company that wants a discovery review of smart-meter data quality. The client wants workshops, data profiling, stakeholder interviews, and a final recommendation in three weeks. The proposal team is tempted to promise an implementation roadmap, a vendor shortlist, and a business case to make the proposal look stronger.

(A) What proposal sections should be included in the response? (3 marks)

(B) What engagement lifecycle stages will this proposal shape later, and how? (3 marks)

(C) Explain three delivery risks created by overpromising in the proposal and how to control them. (4 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]] for proposal structure and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 03/Lecture|Week 03 Lecture]] for the engagement lifecycle.

How to answer: connect proposal wording to later delivery. Scope, methodology, assumptions, pricing, timeline, deliverables, review cycles, and exclusions should be explicit. Overpromising can create scope creep, quality risk, budget pressure, relationship damage, and close-out disputes.

## Q4. Risk

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are a risk analyst reviewing a project for MedEquip Direct, which sells medical devices online. Customer orders, payment references, warranty records, and service notes are held across several disconnected systems. The project team plans to migrate everything into a new platform over one weekend. No rollback plan has been documented. The vendor says it has done similar migrations but has not yet seen the client's data. The client sponsor says downtime is unacceptable because hospitals order replacement parts at short notice.

(A) Define risk as used in this subject. (1 mark)

(B) What are four primary ways to manage risk? Offer a short definition of each. (4 marks)

(C) Identify and discuss five risks in the scenario. Explain why each is a risk and what consequence it may have. (5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]].

How to answer: use scenario facts, not generic IT worries. Consider data quality, cutover timing, missing rollback, vendor knowledge gap, unacceptable downtime, patient or hospital impact, disconnected source systems, and governance over go-live decisions.

## Q5. Governance

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

NovaBuild is a software company that wants to bid for government infrastructure projects. Its culture is informal: any product lead can approve architecture decisions, customer data is used in demos if it helps sales, and delivery teams decide their own security practices. The company has no project board, no formal sign-off authority, and no documented escalation path. The CEO says governance will slow down innovation.

(A) Define project governance as used in the course. (2 marks)

(B) Identify three aspects of NovaBuild that would benefit from organisational or project governance. (3 marks)

(C) Using your knowledge of risk, explain how a governance framework would support risk management in those aspects. (5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]] and the governance answer pattern in [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]].

How to answer: define governance through ownership, roles, status reporting, decision-making, and escalation. Then link governance to risks: security, data use, architecture consistency, tender eligibility, delivery accountability, sign-off, and escalation.

## Q6. Closing Out, Advice, and Follow-On Work

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

Your team has completed a short discovery engagement for Atlas Retail on replacing its workforce scheduling system. The final report recommends a phased implementation because store managers need training and payroll integration requires testing. The sponsor says the board wants a more ambitious single-stage deployment recommendation because it sounds decisive. The sponsor also asks whether your firm can start implementation next week without a new proposal.

(A) What makes the final report professional advice rather than just information or analysis? (3 marks)

(B) What close-out steps should the consulting team complete before the discovery engagement is finished? (4 marks)

(C) How should the consultant handle the request to start implementation next week? (3 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 05/Lecture|Week 05 Lecture]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 07/Lecture|Week 07 Lecture]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/Lecture|Week 11 Lecture]], and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 06/Lecture|Week 06 Lecture]].

How to answer: show the chain from evidence to recommendation, including assumptions, trade-offs, risk, and actionability. Close out the discovery scope formally, then treat implementation as a new or changed engagement requiring scope, price, timeline, assumptions, governance, and sign-off.
