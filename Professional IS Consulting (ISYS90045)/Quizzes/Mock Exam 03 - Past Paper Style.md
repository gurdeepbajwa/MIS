## Q1. Unstructured Problem Solving and Hypotheses

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

Harbour College has received complaints that students cannot get timely academic support. The provost believes the college needs an AI chatbot to answer student questions. Academic advisers say the problem is that policies are inconsistent across schools. Students say they receive different answers depending on who they ask. IT says the existing knowledge base is outdated. The student union says vulnerable students need human support, not automation.

(A) Is the college facing a structured or unstructured problem? Justify your answer. (2 marks)

(B) What problem-solving framework would you use, and how would each step apply? (6 marks)

(C) State one testable working hypothesis you could use to focus early evidence collection. (2 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/Lecture|Week 10 Lecture]] for structured versus unstructured problems and the six-step process. Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 09/Lecture|Week 09 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 09/How to Use Hypothesis-Driven Consulting|How to Use Hypothesis-Driven Consulting]] for hypotheses.

How to answer: treat the chatbot as one possible option, not the problem definition. A good hypothesis is testable and provisional, such as "delays are primarily caused by inconsistent policy interpretation rather than channel availability".

## Q2. Engagement Lifecycle and Bid Judgement

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

Your firm is considering whether to bid for work with AeroParts, a manufacturer that wants a new inventory and supplier-risk dashboard before a major audit. The client wants a fixed fee, a four-week timeline, and a commitment that the dashboard will be production-ready. Your firm has strong business analysts but no available data engineers for the first two weeks. The client also wants the final report to be shared with external auditors.

(A) Name the five stages of the engagement lifecycle. (2.5 marks)

(B) Explain the activities and issues your firm should consider at each stage before, during, and after the AeroParts engagement. (7.5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 03/Lecture|Week 03 Lecture]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]], and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 06/Lecture|Week 06 Lecture]] for negotiation around scope, price, time, and assumptions.

How to answer: include bid/no-bid logic, capability, reliance on limited specialists, external use of the report, fixed-fee scope, contractual negotiation, delivery governance, and close-out quality review.

## Q3. Report Writing and Professional Advice

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are reviewing a draft report for a client, BrightSteps Aged Care. The consulting team investigated why new rostering software has not improved staffing coverage. The draft report has thirty pages of interview summaries, meeting dates, and screenshots of the system. The executive summary says "more work is required". The recommendations section lists ten generic improvements but does not connect them to evidence, trade-offs, or implementation constraints.

(A) What is the difference between information, analysis, and advice? Apply the distinction to the draft report. (3 marks)

(B) What sections should a useful consulting report include? (3 marks)

(C) Explain how you would revise the report so it provides professional advice rather than a diary of work completed. (4 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 05/Lecture|Week 05 Lecture]] for professional advice and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 07/Lecture|Week 07 Lecture]] for report structure and common report mistakes.

How to answer: do not only list report sections. Explain how evidence becomes findings, how findings become recommendations, and how recommendations should include assumptions, trade-offs, risk, and next steps.

## Q4. Risk and Project Governance

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

QuantumDesk sells workplace booking software. It is implementing a new analytics feature for a large enterprise client. The project has no steering committee because the client sponsor says "we can just message each other". Developers release changes directly into production after informal approvals. The project manager has not documented scope exclusions. The client expects daily adoption reports, but the data team says the reporting logic has not been validated. No one knows who can approve a launch delay.

(A) Define project governance as used in this subject. (2 marks)

(B) Identify three aspects of the scenario that would benefit from stronger governance. (3 marks)

(C) Using your knowledge of risk, explain how a governance framework would support risk management in those aspects. (5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]] and the risk and governance section of [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]].

How to answer: define governance through ownership, roles, responsibilities, status updates, decision-making, and escalation. Then link governance controls to risk: scope, quality, launch timing, data validity, production change control, and accountability.

## Q5. Proposals and Proposal Structure

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are preparing a proposal for Riverline Transit, which wants a six-week discovery project on replacing paper-based maintenance logs for buses and trams. The client expects interviews with mechanics, depot managers, IT, procurement, and safety staff. The proposal must explain the work approach, timeline, assumptions, pricing, and relevant experience.

(A) What does a proposal need to prove to a client? (2 marks)

(B) Identify the major sections of a consulting proposal and explain what each should cover for Riverline Transit. (5 marks)

(C) Identify three proposal assumptions or exclusions that should be made explicit to reduce later delivery risk. (3 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]] and the proposal section of [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]].

How to answer: focus on client confidence and delivery control. Assumptions might cover number of interviews, access to depots, availability of client staff, no implementation build in discovery scope, data access, travel, and review cycles.

## Q6. Conflict and Close-Out

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

Near the end of an engagement for Helio Energy, the consulting team finds that the client cannot achieve the promised benefits unless operations changes its maintenance planning process. The operations director says the finding is unfair and refuses to attend the final presentation. The sponsor asks you to "avoid stirring things up" and provide a softer version of the report. There are also unresolved risks about data quality and ownership of next steps after the consultants leave.

(A) What type or types of conflict might be present? Use scenario evidence. (2 marks)

(B) How should the consultant manage the difficult conversation with the operations director and sponsor? (4 marks)

(C) What close-out activities should be completed so the engagement ends responsibly? (4 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/Lecture|Week 11 Lecture]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/How to Manage Consulting Conflict|How to Manage Consulting Conflict]], and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/How to Close Out a Consulting Engagement|How to Close Out a Consulting Engagement]].

How to answer: separate people, behaviour, issue, and decision. Use neutral evidence, listen for interests, explain trade-offs, and escalate through governance if needed. For close-out, mention final review, residual risk documentation, sign-off, handover, and ownership of follow-on actions.
