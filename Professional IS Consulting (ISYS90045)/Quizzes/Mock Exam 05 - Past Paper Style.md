## Q1. Unstructured Problem Solving

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

BlueHarbour Health operates outpatient clinics across regional areas. Patients complain that appointment wait times are unpredictable. Clinic managers say specialist availability is the issue. Reception staff say the booking system creates duplicate appointments and does not show cancellations quickly. The finance director says the organisation should outsource appointment scheduling to a call-centre provider to reduce labour cost. The clinical director says outsourcing could weaken patient triage and continuity of care. The CEO asks your firm whether BlueHarbour should outsource appointment scheduling.

(A) Is this a structured or unstructured problem? Why? (1 mark)

(B) What framework would you use to help BlueHarbour understand and solve the problem? Identify each step and outline how each step would apply. (9 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/Lecture|Week 10 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process|How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process]].

How to answer: treat outsourcing as one option, not the problem definition. Select criteria before alternatives. Possible criteria include patient access, triage safety, continuity of care, staff workload, cost, implementation risk, time to value, and system reliability.

## Q2. Proposal Commitments and Engagement Lifecycle

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

CityGrid Transport wants a four-week discovery review of asset-maintenance data quality across bus depots. The client asks for stakeholder interviews, sample data profiling, a workshop with depot managers, and a final recommendation. A partner suggests adding "implementation support and dashboard prototyping if time permits" to make the proposal more attractive.

(A) What proposal sections should be included in the response? (3 marks)

(B) Explain how this proposal will shape later engagement lifecycle stages. (3 marks)

(C) Identify three delivery risks created by vague optional promises such as "if time permits", and explain how to control them. (4 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 03/Lecture|Week 03 Lecture]].

How to answer: connect proposal wording to contract terms, delivery governance, scope control, pricing, assumptions, review cycles, and close-out sign-off.

## Q3. Meetings, Interviews, and Workshops

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

Northbank University wants to improve how students request disability and academic adjustments. Students say the process is slow and inconsistent. Faculty administrators say forms are incomplete. Student advisers say some cases are sensitive and should not be discussed in large groups. The deputy provost wants one large workshop with students, academics, administrators, and advisers to "get everything on the table".

(A) Explain when a consultant should use a meeting, an interview, and a workshop. (3 marks)

(B) Design an initial engagement plan using at least one meeting, one interview activity, and one workshop activity. Explain the purpose of each. (4 marks)

(C) Identify three facilitation or stakeholder risks in the deputy provost's large-workshop request and explain how you would manage each risk. (3 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 08/Lecture|Week 08 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 07/Lecture|Week 07 Lecture]].

How to answer: match the format to the evidence need. Sensitive student experiences may require private interviews before a workshop validates themes, maps process issues, or prioritises improvements.

## Q4. Risk Treatment and Risk Register Thinking

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

SafeMeals Online sells prepared meals to aged-care providers. Customer orders, allergy notes, delivery records, payment references, and service complaints are stored across a legacy ordering tool, spreadsheets, and a shared inbox. The project team wants to migrate all records to a new platform in one weekend. The vendor has not reviewed real client data. No rollback plan has been tested. The operations sponsor says delivery interruption is unacceptable because aged-care homes rely on daily meal deliveries.

(A) Define risk as used in this subject. (1 mark)

(B) What are four primary ways to manage risk? Offer a short definition of each. (4 marks)

(C) Identify and discuss five risks in the scenario. For each risk, explain the scenario evidence and possible consequence. (5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]].

How to answer: use a risk-register pattern: risk, evidence, consequence, mitigation, owner or escalation. Do not accept high-impact downtime casually.

## Q5. Governance

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

TenderCloud is a software company preparing to bid for public-sector procurement work. Product teams approve their own production releases. Sales staff use screenshots containing customer data in demonstrations. Security controls differ between teams. There is no project board, no risk register, no formal sign-off authority, and no documented escalation path. The CEO says governance will slow the company down.

(A) Define project governance as used in the course. (2 marks)

(B) Identify three aspects of TenderCloud that would benefit from organisational or project governance. (3 marks)

(C) Using your knowledge of risk, explain how a governance framework would support risk management in those aspects. (5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]].

How to answer: use the governance vocabulary directly: ownership, roles, decision rights, reporting, escalation, approval, and sign-off. Link each control to a risk consequence.

## Q6. Professional Advice, Close-Out, and Follow-On Work

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

Your team has completed a discovery engagement for MetroFit on replacing its payroll and rostering system. The final report recommends a phased implementation because payroll integration needs testing and store managers need training. The sponsor says the board wants a single-stage deployment recommendation because it sounds stronger. The sponsor also asks the consulting team to keep making small changes to the final report and to begin implementation planning next week without a new proposal.

(A) What makes the final report professional advice rather than just information or analysis? (3 marks)

(B) What close-out steps should the consulting team complete before the discovery engagement is finished? (4 marks)

(C) How should the consultant handle the request to start implementation planning next week? (3 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 05/Lecture|Week 05 Lecture]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/Lecture|Week 11 Lecture]], and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 06/Lecture|Week 06 Lecture]].

How to answer: preserve professional advice quality. Formal close-out should include QA/risk review, final iterations, sign-off, residual risks, handover, ownership of next steps, and lessons learned. Follow-on work needs scope, price, timeline, assumptions, governance, contract terms, and sign-off.

### Q1 Marking

Score: 8/10

Rubric:

- Part A, structured versus unstructured problem (1 mark): full credit for identifying the problem as unstructured and explaining that causes, criteria, solution path, and consequences are uncertain or contested.
- Part B, IESE six-step process (9 marks): full credit for explicitly applying problem definition, criteria selection, alternative development, alternative analysis, decision, and action planning to BlueHarbour.

Feedback:

- Correctly classified the issue as unstructured. The answer recognised that wait times can be measured but the root cause and best solution are uncertain because the scenario includes specialist capacity, system defects, patient service, cost, triage, and continuity-of-care tensions.
- The IESE steps were much clearer than in the previous mock. The answer defined the problem, selected criteria, developed alternatives, analysed trade-offs, made a recommendation, and gave next steps.
- The criteria were relevant and scenario-specific: wait times, booking error rates, cancellation visibility, cost, triage, and continuity of care. A stronger answer would also explicitly include implementation risk, time to value, patient safety, staff workload, and system reliability.
- The analysis compared outsourcing against an internal booking-system improvement and explained the clinical risks of outsourcing. This was a good use of scenario facts.
- The main limitation was alternative breadth. The answer mostly compared two options. A stronger exam answer would add options such as process redesign, specialist-capacity management, scheduling rules, staged platform uplift, hybrid overflow support, or a pilot before full rollout.
- The action plan was useful, but could be more complete by adding owners, sequencing, decision gates, risk controls, success measures, and a follow-up review period.

Weak areas:

- Broaden alternatives beyond two options.
- Turn next steps into a fuller action plan with owners, sequence, risks, measures, and governance.
- Be careful with problem wording: "poor service" is a useful symptom phrase, but the problem definition should stay neutral until evidence confirms the root cause.

Revision target:

- Keep using the explicit IESE structure. For the next problem-solving answer, add at least three alternatives and compare them against the criteria before recommending.

Rubric:

- Part B, engagement lifecycle (3 marks): full credit for explaining how the proposal shapes contract negotiation, delivery governance, scope control, pricing, assumptions, review points, and close-out sign-off.
- Part C, vague optional-promise risks (4 marks): full credit for identifying three delivery risks from "implementation support and dashboard prototyping if time permits" and pairing each risk with a concrete control.

Feedback:

- Some credit was given for recognising that the work is a discovery engagement and that implementation should be treated separately.

- Explaining proposal-to-delivery obligations.
- Listing three complete risks and controls.

### Q3 Marking

Score: 7/10

Rubric:

- Part A, when to use each format (3 marks): full credit for distinguishing meetings as agenda-driven governance, alignment, decision, or status sessions; interviews as deeper evidence-gathering and perspective-testing conversations; and workshops as collaborative sessions for shared understanding, requirements, design, prioritisation, review, or problem solving.
- Part B, initial engagement plan (4 marks): full credit for sequencing at least one meeting, interview activity, and workshop activity, with each activity linked to Northbank and a clear purpose.
- Part C, facilitation and stakeholder risks (3 marks): full credit for identifying three risks in the large-workshop request and explaining how each risk would be managed.

Feedback:

- The distinctions between meetings, interviews, and workshops were mostly correct. Meetings were linked to updates, approvals, and direction; interviews were linked to deeper context; workshops were linked to group collaboration and engagement.
- The engagement plan was logical: leadership meeting first, then interviews, then a workshop to discuss findings and options.
- The answer correctly recognised that student advisers and administrative staff should be interviewed separately so they can speak more openly.
- The main missing stakeholder was students. Because students are the affected users and some cases are sensitive, student interviews or carefully designed confidential input should be included.
- The three risks in Part C were relevant: too many people derailing the workshop, sensitive issues being discussed in public, and senior leadership intimidating staff. The mark loss is that the answer did not explain enough controls for each risk.
- Stronger controls would include private interviews before the workshop, anonymised synthesis of sensitive themes, smaller breakout groups, separate stakeholder workshops where needed, a tight agenda, clear outputs, and facilitation rules.

Weak areas:

- Turning each facilitation risk into a management action.
- Including affected users, not just staff and leadership.
- Being more precise about workshop outputs such as validated themes, process maps, prioritised issues, or agreed solution options.

Revision target:

### Q4 Marking

Score: 8/10

Rubric:

- Part A, risk definition (1 mark): full credit for defining risk as uncertainty that may affect objectives, including delivery, scope, cost, schedule, quality, stakeholder acceptance, legal exposure, technical feasibility, trust, or reputation.
- Part B, four primary risk treatments (4 marks): full credit for correctly defining avoid, mitigate or reduce, transfer or share, and accept.
- Part C, five scenario risks (5 marks): full credit for identifying five specific risks from the SafeMeals scenario, explaining the scenario evidence, and stating the possible consequence.

Feedback:

- The risk definition was broadly correct. It captured negative project outcomes and the need to identify risks during planning. A stronger answer would use the course wording directly: risk is uncertainty that may affect objectives.
- The four treatment options were well defined. Avoid, mitigate, accept, and transfer were all explained in a way that would receive strong credit.
- The five risks were relevant and grounded in the scenario: untested rollback, vendor not reviewing client data, delivery interruption, fragmented source systems, and the single-weekend migration window.
- The answer improved on the previous mock by treating delivery interruption as a high-impact risk that should be avoided or mitigated, not casually accepted.
- The main weakness was the vendor-risk treatment. The vendor having responsibility does not fully transfer the risk away from the client or consulting team. The risk still needs due diligence, data profiling, access to sample data, contractual assumptions, governance oversight, and escalation if readiness is poor.
- The data risks could be more specific. Allergy notes, payment references, complaints, and delivery records each have different consequences, such as safety incidents, billing errors, service failures, or loss of complaint history.

Weak areas:

- Using the course risk definition exactly.
- Recognising that risk transfer still requires oversight and governance.
- Making consequences more specific to each data type.

Revision target:

- Keep using the risk-register structure: risk, evidence, consequence, mitigation, and owner or escalation path. For transfer risks, add the governance mechanism that checks whether the third party is actually managing the risk.
