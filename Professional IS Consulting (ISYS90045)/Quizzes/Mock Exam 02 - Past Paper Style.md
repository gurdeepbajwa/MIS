## Q1. Unstructured Problem Solving

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

FreshField Grocers is a family-owned supermarket chain with twelve stores. The owner has noticed declining customer loyalty and believes the solution is to launch a mobile app with digital coupons. The operations manager argues that the problem is poor shelf availability. The finance manager says margins are falling because suppliers keep changing prices. Store staff say customers are frustrated by long checkout queues. A competitor has recently opened stores with self-checkout, local produce ranges, and online ordering.

The owner asks your consulting team: should FreshField invest in a mobile app to protect sales and customer loyalty?

(A) Explain the key difference between structured and unstructured problems. Apply that distinction to FreshField. (2 marks)

(B) Use the course problem-solving approach to show how you would help the owner move from this ambiguous question to a defensible recommendation. (8 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/Lecture|Week 10 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process|How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process]].

How to answer: define structured versus unstructured using goals, information, routine process, and judgement. Then walk through the six-step process. Make the alternatives broader than "app or no app": process improvement, checkout redesign, stock visibility, supplier analysis, loyalty changes, or phased digital investment.

## Q2. Stakeholder Engagement

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, C, and D.

You are a newly promoted manager at a consulting firm. A partner sends you to help CivicWorks, a local council, decide whether to implement a new case-management platform for resident service requests. The mayor wants a public-facing dashboard. The customer service team wants fewer duplicate complaints. Field crews want clearer work orders. IT is worried about integration with older systems. Residents have complained publicly that requests disappear without updates.

(A) How do consultants define meetings, and why do consultants have them? (2 marks)

(B) How would you establish a useful meeting culture for this engagement? (2 marks)

(C) Describe four interview types or formats and explain how social cues affect each. (4 marks)

(D) Identify four stakeholders you would interview and justify the interview type you would use for each. (2 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 08/Lecture|Week 08 Lecture]] for meetings, meeting culture, OPQRST, interview formats, and social cues. Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 04/Lecture|Week 04 Lecture]] for audience-aware communication.

How to answer: do not describe generic "talking to people". For each stakeholder, identify what evidence they hold and what interview format protects the quality of that evidence. Mention social cues, anonymity, cost, timing, power dynamics, and whether the topic is sensitive.

## Q3. Engagement Lifecycle

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

Your consulting firm has heard that LumiBank, a small digital bank, wants support with a payments modernisation project. The client wants faster payments, better fraud monitoring, and integration with a new customer app. The opportunity is commercially attractive, but your firm has limited payments specialists available for the proposed dates. The client also wants the work completed before a public product launch.

(A) What are five key stages in the engagement lifecycle? (2.5 marks)

(B) What key activities would your firm perform in each stage, and why are they important for LumiBank? (7.5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 03/Lecture|Week 03 Lecture]] and the engagement lifecycle section of [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]].

How to answer: apply each stage to risk, capability, proposal commitments, contract scope, delivery governance, and close-out. Use the facts about payments expertise, launch deadline, fraud monitoring, and integration risk.

## Q4. Closing the Engagement and Project Failure

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

You are a director at ClearPath Consulting. One of your managers has completed a process review for Portside Medical Imaging, but the client has complained that the final deliverable does not match the Statement of Work. The client says the report describes interview themes but does not provide the agreed implementation roadmap. You ask the manager for close-out documents, acceptance records, open issue logs, and lessons learned. The manager replies that the team "sent the final deck and moved on".

(A) List five consequences of project failure or poor close-out. (2.5 marks)

(B) List five reports, records, or close-out outputs that should be generated at the end of an engagement. Describe what each one is for. (7.5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/Lecture|Week 11 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/How to Close Out a Consulting Engagement|How to Close Out a Consulting Engagement]].

How to answer: distinguish consequences from artefacts. Consequences can include client dissatisfaction, payment dispute, reputational damage, lost follow-on work, legal exposure, unused deliverables, or unresolved risks. Artefacts can include analytical report, lessons learned report, follow-on action recommendations, ROI report, end-of-project report, sign-off record, issue log, or handover pack.

## Q5. Proposals

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

You are on a pursuit team at Qualis Advisory. A manufacturing company, MakerOne, has released several opportunities. One is a government-funded tender for cybersecurity uplift across factories. Another is a privately negotiated request from the CFO for a short review of finance-system reporting. A third is an informal request from the CIO for market information about asset-tracking platforms before the company decides whether to run a formal procurement process.

(A) List five proposal request types or sales methods you might encounter across public and private engagements. Provide a basic description of each. (7.5 marks)

(B) List the sections you would expect to see in most consulting proposals. (2.5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]] for RFI, EOI, RFQ, RFT, RFP, proposal sections, and understanding the audience.

How to answer: match each request type to a procurement situation. Then list proposal sections cleanly. Do not blur an RFI with an RFP: one seeks information; the other asks for a proposed solution.

## Q6. Risk and Governance

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are an IT risk consultant reviewing OrbitalFit, a fitness subscription company that tracks customer health goals and payment data. The company lets product teams create their own cloud services using company cards. No one has a complete list of systems or data stores. Customer data is copied into spreadsheets for marketing analysis. The head of product approves changes directly with developers, while customer support is not told when changes affect the app. There is no project board, no risk register, and no documented decision-making process.

(A) Define risk. (1 mark)

(B) What are five primary ways of managing risk? Provide a short definition of each. (5 marks)

(C) Identify and discuss four risks in the OrbitalFit notes. Explain why each is a risk and describe its inherent consequence. (4 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]], especially risk and governance, and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]].

How to answer: define risk as uncertainty affecting objectives, then apply risk responses. For part C, use evidence from the facts: uncontrolled cloud procurement, unknown data stores, spreadsheet copies of customer data, informal approval, poor change communication, absent governance, and absent risk register.
