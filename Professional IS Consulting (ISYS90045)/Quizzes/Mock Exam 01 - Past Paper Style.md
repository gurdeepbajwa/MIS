## Q1. Unstructured Problem Solving

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

You are a consultant at Northbridge Advisory. A mid-sized homewares retailer, Hearth & Hall, has experienced falling in-store revenue despite opening three new stores in the last year. The executive team thinks the issue is that customers now expect personalised online recommendations, click-and-collect options, and same-day delivery. The COO argues that Hearth & Hall should immediately buy an expensive customer data platform and integrate it with the point-of-sale system. The CFO is worried about cost and says the company should close two stores instead. Store managers say the real issue is poor stock visibility and frequent out-of-stock items. The marketing team says the brand has become less distinctive and is attracting fewer younger customers.

The CEO asks your consulting team to answer this question: should Hearth & Hall invest in a customer data platform to reverse the sales decline?

(A) Is this a structured or unstructured problem? Why? (1 mark)

(B) Which course problem-solving framework would you use to help the client understand and respond to the problem? Identify each step and explain how it would apply to Hearth & Hall. (9 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/Lecture|Week 10 Lecture]] and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 10/How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process|How to Apply the IESE Six-Step Problem-Solving Process]]. If you want extra structure, also review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 09/How to Use Hypothesis-Driven Consulting|How to Use Hypothesis-Driven Consulting]].

How to answer: do not jump straight to "buy the platform" or "close stores". First classify the problem using uncertainty, contested goals, missing information, and strategic consequences. Then apply the six steps: define the real problem, choose criteria, develop alternatives, analyse trade-offs, decide, and create an action plan.

## Q2. Engagement Lifecycle

Read the following scenario and answer BOTH embedded questions: A and B.

You are a senior consultant at a firm that has just noticed a procurement notice from MetroCare Clinics. MetroCare operates twenty suburban clinics and wants support selecting and implementing a new appointment scheduling and patient reminder system. The notice says the current system causes double-bookings, missed appointments, frustrated reception staff, and poor visibility for clinicians. It also says the client wants "a rapid result with minimal disruption" and expects the successful consultant to coordinate with the software vendor, clinic managers, and the executive sponsor.

Your partner asks you to advise whether the firm should pursue the work and, if so, how the engagement would move from opportunity to close-out.

(A) What are five key stages in the consulting engagement lifecycle? (2.5 marks)

(B) What key activities should occur in each stage, and how are those activities relevant to MetroCare Clinics? (7.5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 03/Lecture|Week 03 Lecture]] for the engagement lifecycle and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]] for proposal and bid judgement.

How to answer: name the five stages first, then apply each to the scenario. For each stage, state the activity and the risk it controls. For example, risk assessment should consider healthcare disruption, capability, client reputation, and whether the firm can deliver with the vendor and clinic stakeholders.

## Q3. Stakeholder Engagement, Meetings, and Interviews

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, C, and D.

GreenRoute Logistics wants to redesign the way it manages driver schedules, depot loading times, and customer delivery updates. The executive sponsor believes the work is mainly a system problem. Depot supervisors say the problem is that planners change routes too late in the day. Drivers say they are blamed for delays caused by warehouse bottlenecks. Customer service staff say they lack accurate delivery information and receive angry calls from customers. The sponsor has asked you to begin information gathering next week.

(A) Why do consultants use meetings in engagements, and what makes a meeting useful rather than wasteful? (2 marks)

(B) What is an agenda, and what agenda-preparation tool from the course could you use? Describe the main features of the tool. (2 marks)

(C) Describe four interview formats or types and explain one strength or limitation of each. (4 marks)

(D) Identify four stakeholders you would interview for GreenRoute Logistics. For each, choose an interview type and justify the choice. (2 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 08/Lecture|Week 08 Lecture]] for meetings, agendas, interviews, social cues, anonymity, and stakeholder engagement. Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 07/Lecture|Week 07 Lecture]] if you want to distinguish interviews from workshops.

How to answer: show that meetings, interviews, and workshops are different tools. In part D, choose stakeholders based on evidence needs, not hierarchy alone. Strong answers justify why a driver might need a confidential one-on-one interview while a route-planning workshop might suit process mapping after initial interviews.

## Q4. Risk and Governance

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are asked to review early project notes for EduVault, a small education technology company that stores student records and assessment data for several private colleges. EduVault has grown quickly and now wants to expand overseas. Its three largest clients all share the same cloud environment because the founder says "separate environments are expensive and slow us down". Backups are performed manually by one senior engineer, Priya, who is also the only person with administrator access to the production database. There is no formal incident response plan, and client-facing staff promise uptime targets in sales calls even though there are no documented service levels. The project team has no clear escalation path for scope, budget, data-security, or client-impact issues.

(A) Define risk as used in this subject. (1 mark)

(B) What are four primary ways to manage risk? Give a short definition of each. (4 marks)

(C) Identify and discuss five risks in the EduVault notes. For each risk, explain the scenario evidence and the possible consequence. (5 marks)

### Hint

Review the risk and governance section of [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Exam/PISC Exam Cheat Sheet|PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Assets/Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief.pdf|Week 10 - AT2 Risk and Governance Brief]], and the project management material linked from Week 09.

How to answer: use a risk-register pattern: risk, evidence, consequence, mitigation or owner. Do not list vague risks such as "the system is bad". Anchor each risk in a concrete fact: shared cloud tenancy, single-person dependency, privileged access, manual backup, undocumented service levels, or absent escalation.

## Q5. Proposals and Selling Consulting Services

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are a manager at DataBridge Consulting. A regional university has issued an RFP for a four-week discovery engagement on whether to replace its student enquiry management system. The current process relies on shared inboxes, manual spreadsheet tracking, and informal follow-ups by faculty administrators. The client wants interviews with admissions, student services, faculty staff, and IT, plus a short report recommending improvement options. Your partner asks you to draft the proposal response.

(A) What does RFP stand for, and what does it mean in consulting procurement? Name other proposal request types or sales methods discussed in class and define them briefly. (2 marks)

(B) What sections would you typically include in a proposal of this kind, and what should each section cover? (3 marks)

(C) The course discussed two methods of selling consulting services. Which method would you use in this response, and how would you apply it to the university scenario? (5 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 02/Lecture|Week 02 Lecture]] for proposal types, proposal structure, understanding the audience, and bid/no-bid judgement. Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 03/Lecture|Week 03 Lecture]] for how proposals connect to later delivery risk.

How to answer: separate procurement vocabulary from proposal design. For part B, cover executive summary, introduction, understanding of needs, methodology and timeline, pricing and assumptions, team CVs, and similar work. For part C, explain how the selling method would show client understanding rather than just advertise the firm.

## Q6. Closing Out the Engagement

Read the following scenario and answer ALL embedded questions: A, B, and C.

You are the engagement manager for a project that reviewed the billing and account-management process at Streamline Utilities. The team has completed interviews, analysed complaints data, and drafted a final report. The report finds that billing errors are caused by inconsistent handoffs between sales, operations, and finance. The client sponsor likes the recommendations but asks you to remove references to finance because the CFO is sensitive about criticism. One department head also wants the consulting team to provide two extra implementation workshops after sign-off, even though workshops were not included in the signed scope.

(A) What activities and steps are needed to close out the engagement successfully? (5 marks)

(B) Should the partner or senior leader review the final report before it goes to the client? Why or why not? (2 marks)

(C) Identify three consequences if the project fails or closes poorly. (3 marks)

### Hint

Review [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/Lecture|Week 11 Lecture]], [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/How to Close Out a Consulting Engagement|How to Close Out a Consulting Engagement]], and [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Week 11/How to Manage Consulting Conflict|How to Manage Consulting Conflict]].

How to answer: include both delivery mechanics and relationship management. Mention final review, quality or legal checks, final presentation, iterations, user or sponsor acceptance, sign-off, residual risks, handover, and lessons learned. Treat the sponsor's wording request and extra workshops as close-out, conflict, and scope-control issues.
