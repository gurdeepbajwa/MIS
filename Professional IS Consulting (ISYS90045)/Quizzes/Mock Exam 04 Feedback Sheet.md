## Overall Diagnosis

The answers usually identified the correct concept and used relevant scenario facts. Most lost marks came from not making the course framework explicit enough, not pairing risks with controls, and not using formal consulting vocabulary for governance, proposal delivery, and close-out.

## Weak Areas To Fix

| Weak area | Where it showed up | What to do next time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framework steps were implied rather than explicit | Q1 IESE problem solving | Name each step: define problem, select criteria, develop alternatives, analyse alternatives, decide, create action plan. |
| Alternatives were not compared clearly against criteria | Q1 IESE problem solving | Use the selected criteria to compare each option before recommending. Treat scenario claims as hypotheses, not proof. |
| Facilitation risks needed concrete controls | Q2 meetings, interviews, workshops | For each risk, add a management action such as separate interviews, anonymised synthesis, smaller groups, tight agenda, or clear workshop outputs. |
| Proposal promises were not fully connected to delivery risk | Q3 proposal and lifecycle | Explain how proposal wording becomes a contract, delivery, scope-control, and close-out obligation. |
| Risk treatment judgement was too soft on high-impact risks | Q4 risk | Do not accept high-impact operational risks casually. Propose mitigation, avoidance, escalation, or go/no-go governance. |
| Risk answers needed the full risk-register pattern | Q4 risk | Write: risk, evidence, consequence, mitigation, owner or escalation path. |
| Governance vocabulary was incomplete | Q5 governance | Use ownership, roles, decision rights, reporting, escalation, approval, and sign-off. |
| Governance controls were too general | Q5 governance | Name concrete controls: project board, architecture review, security standards, risk register, data-use approval, escalation path, sign-off authority. |
| Close-out mechanics were not formal enough | Q6 close-out | Name QA/risk review, final iterations, client sign-off, residual risks, handover, ownership transfer, support arrangements, and lessons learned. |
| Client pressure was not always separated from evidence | Q6 professional advice | Do not change a recommendation just because it sounds more decisive. Change it only if evidence, risk, or constraints justify it. |

## Strong Areas To Keep

- Correctly identified most tested concepts.
- Used scenario facts rather than giving purely generic answers.
- Distinguished information, analysis, and professional advice well.
- Generally understood that implementation work needs a new or changed engagement.
- Proposal-section recall was stronger than proposal-to-delivery explanation.

## Next Exam Checklist

Before writing, identify the tested concept, the relevant framework, the key scenario facts, the risk or consequence, and the professional action.

Use these sentence patterns where relevant:

- Problem solving: "This is unstructured because..., so I would use IESE: define..., select criteria..., develop alternatives..., analyse..., decide..., action plan..."
- Proposal: "This proposal promise creates a later obligation because it affects contract terms, delivery scope, pricing, assumptions, governance, and close-out acceptance."
- Risk: "The risk is..., the evidence is..., the consequence is..., the mitigation is..., and the owner/escalation path is..."
- Governance: "Governance reduces this risk by assigning ownership, decision rights, reporting, escalation, approval, and sign-off."
- Close-out: "Close-out should confirm scope completion, QA/risk review, final iterations, sign-off, residual risks, handover, ownership of next steps, and lessons learned."
