## Highest Priority Fixes

### Governance

Use this pattern every time:

1. Aspect needing governance.
2. Risk created by weak governance.
3. Governance control.
4. How the control reduces risk.

Sentence pattern:

> The weak governance aspect is [X]. This creates [Y risk] because [scenario evidence]. Governance would assign [ownership/decision rights/reporting/escalation/approval/sign-off]. This reduces risk by [making accountability visible/preventing informal decisions/creating a route for unresolved issues].

Core vocabulary:

- ownership;
- roles and responsibilities;
- decision rights;
- status reporting;
- escalation;
- approval gates;
- sign-off;
- risk register;
- project board or steering forum.

### Engagement Lifecycle

Do not only list the five stages. Use the stages as headings and add scenario facts under each.

| Stage | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Risk assessment/internal inquiry | Capability, client fit, reputation, delivery risk, whether the firm should bid. |
| Winning the work | Proposal, understanding of needs, relevant experience, pricing, assumptions, mandatory requirements. |
| Contract negotiation | Scope, price, timeline, responsibilities, legal terms, assumptions, exclusions, change control, sign-off. |
| Delivery/project management | Kick-off, governance, stakeholder work, testing, vendor coordination, risk register, reporting, escalation. |
| Close-out/QA | Final review, acceptance, handover, residual risks, support limits, lessons learned, invoicing. |

Exam move:

> Add two scenario-specific activities under each stage.

### Risk

Definition:

> Risk is uncertainty that may affect project or business objectives.

Risk responses:

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Avoid | Change the plan so the risk cannot occur. |
| Mitigate/reduce | Lower the likelihood or impact. |
| Transfer | Move responsibility to another party better placed to manage it. |
| Share | Distribute responsibility through partnership, joint governance, or contract structure. |
| Accept | Knowingly carry the risk because it is low impact or too costly to avoid or reduce. |

Scenario-risk pattern:

> Risk: [what might happen]. Evidence: [scenario fact]. Consequence: [why it matters]. Control: [mitigation/owner/escalation].

Never leave Part C blank. Short bullet points can still earn marks.

### Problem Solving

Do not treat the client's preferred system as the answer.

IESE structure:

1. Define the problem neutrally.
2. Select criteria before choosing an option.
3. Develop at least three alternatives.
4. Analyse alternatives against the criteria.
5. Decide with trade-offs.
6. Create an action plan with owners, sequence, risks, measures, and follow-up.

Preferred recommendation when evidence is incomplete:

> Recommend a discovery, pilot, staged rollout, or decision gate before full implementation.

### Proposals

Do not mix proposal sections with final report content.

Proposal answers should include:

- executive summary;
- introduction/background;
- understanding of needs;
- methodology and timeline;
- scope and deliverables;
- pricing;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- governance/review/sign-off points;
- team CVs;
- similar work.

Proposal request types:

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| RFI | Request for information; explores the market. |
| EOI | Expression of interest; tests supplier interest before later procurement. |
| RFQ | Request for quote; asks for pricing, often for standardised services. |
| RFT | Request for tender; formal bid request, common in government. |
| RFP | Request for proposal; asks suppliers to propose a solution. |

### Close-Out

Named report types to remember:

- analytical report;
- lessons learned report;
- follow-on action recommendations;
- ROI report;
- end-of-project report.

Close-out records and artefacts:

- sign-off or acceptance record;
- issue and risk closure log;
- handover pack;
- residual risk register;
- support arrangement.

Exam move:

> If the question asks for reports, list the named report types first. If it asks for close-out activities, include sign-off, handover, residual risks, support, and lessons learned.

### Meetings And Interviews

OPQRST agenda:

- order/objectives;
- process;
- starting question;
- recording method;
- supplies/tools;
- timing.

Stakeholder interview pattern:

> Stakeholder: [person/group]. Evidence needed: [what they know]. Interview type: [face-to-face/video/phone/email]. Reason: [social cues, sensitivity, geography, anonymity, depth, or documentation].

## Last-Minute Exam Checklist

- Have I named the framework?
- Have I used scenario facts, not generic language?
- Have I included controls, not only risks?
- Have I separated proposal from report?
- Have I added sign-off, assumptions, exclusions, and ownership where relevant?
- If evidence is incomplete, have I recommended a pilot or staged decision?
