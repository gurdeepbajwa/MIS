# PISC Flashcard Deck

Course: [[Professional IS Consulting (ISYS90045)/Course Overview|Professional IS Consulting]]
Purpose: active recall for the PISC exam.
Sources: [[PISC Exam Cheat Sheet]], [[PISC 6Cs Exam Study Guide]], [[PISC Past Paper Weakness Cheat Sheet]]

## How to Use

Cover the `Back` and `Exam use` columns before answering. Say the answer aloud in 20-40 seconds, then check whether you named the concept, used course language, and explained the consulting implication.

Use this marking code while studying:

- `Hard`: could not recall the concept or distinction.
- `Partial`: remembered the idea but missed exam language or application.
- `Ready`: can answer clearly and apply it to a scenario.

## Core Spine Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PISC-01 | What are the 6Cs memory spine for PISC? | Client, Commit, Communicate, Consult, Control, Close. | Use these to locate where a scenario is putting pressure on the consulting engagement. |
| PISC-02 | What does `Client` mean in the 6Cs? | Understand who the client really is, why consultants are involved, which stakeholders matter, and what trust issue affects the work. | Use when the scenario involves consultant value, distrust, audience needs, or stakeholder complexity. |
| PISC-03 | What does `Commit` mean in the 6Cs? | Turn a possible opportunity into agreed work through bid judgement, proposal, scope, assumptions, pricing, negotiation, contract terms, and responsibilities. | Use for RFPs, proposals, pricing, scope, faster timelines, extra work, or unclear agreement terms. |
| PISC-04 | What does `Communicate` mean in the 6Cs? | Convert consulting work into advice, reports, findings, recommendations, and presentations the client can understand, trust, and use. | Use for technical-to-management communication, advice, report writing, executive summaries, and recommendation questions. |
| PISC-05 | What does `Consult` mean in the 6Cs? | Gather evidence, choose meetings/interviews/workshops, frame the real problem, test hypotheses, and move from ambiguity to a defensible recommendation. | Use for stakeholder evidence, messy problems, hypothesis-driven consulting, and problem-solving steps. |
| PISC-06 | What does `Control` mean in the 6Cs? | Manage risk, governance, scope, change requests, conflict, difficult stakeholders, ownership, and escalation. | Use when the scenario shows unmanaged risk, unclear authority, resistance, scope creep, or conflict. |
| PISC-07 | What does `Close` mean in the 6Cs? | Complete the engagement formally through acceptance, sign-off, handover, residual risks, support arrangements, lessons learned, and future actions. | Use for ending, disputed completion, final report, handover, or follow-on work scenarios. |
| PISC-08 | What is the core PISC exam answer rule? | Do not definition-dump. Identify the consulting issue, name and define the concept, apply scenario facts, explain the implication, and recommend the next professional action. | Use as the checklist for every written answer. |
| PISC-09 | What course vocabulary should appear in strong PISC answers? | Proposal, engagement, scope, assumption, deliverable, stakeholder, sponsor, advice, risk, finding, recommendation, governance, sign-off. | Use these words to keep answers in professional consulting language. |
| PISC-10 | What is the combined 6Cs answer pattern? | Locate the engagement point, name the concept, use scenario evidence, explain the consulting implication, recommend the professional action. | Use for mixed questions that cross several topics. |

## Consulting Role And Client Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PISC-11 | Why do organisations hire consultants? | For expertise, independence, external credibility, temporary capacity, structured methods, and professional judgement. | Do not say consultants simply "fix" the client's problem. |
| PISC-12 | What does the client still own, even when consultants advise? | Decisions, organisational change, adoption, and consequences of action. | Use this to avoid overstating consultant authority. |
| PISC-13 | What are the four trust components? | Credibility, reliability, intimacy, and low self-orientation. | If trust is weak, identify which component is weak and why. |
| PISC-14 | How should you treat "the client" in an exam scenario? | As an ecosystem: sponsor, decision makers, users, IT, procurement, executives, affected staff, and governance bodies may have different needs. | Avoid treating the client as one unified voice. |
| PISC-15 | What should a consultant clarify before acting? | Objective, success criteria, constraints, stakeholder access, authority, expectations, and decision rights. | Use when a scenario begins with ambiguity or an informal request. |

## Proposal And Engagement Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PISC-16 | What is a proposal? | A written offer explaining the client problem, proposed services, approach, budget, team, assumptions, and relevant evidence of capability. | Treat it as a delivery-risk control, not just a sales pitch. |
| PISC-17 | What belongs in a strong proposal? | Executive summary, introduction/background, understanding of needs, methodology and timeline, scope and deliverables, pricing, assumptions/exclusions, governance or sign-off points, team CVs, and similar work. | Use this as the proposal-section recall list. |
| PISC-18 | RFI, EOI, RFQ, RFT, RFP: what are they? | RFI explores market information; EOI tests supplier interest; RFQ asks for pricing; RFT is a formal tender request; RFP asks suppliers to propose a solution. | Name the request type when the question gives procurement language. |
| PISC-19 | Executive summary vs introduction? | Executive summary is decision-focused and states the main offer, recommendation, or conclusion; introduction gives background, context, scope, and structure. | Useful for report or proposal-writing questions. |
| PISC-20 | What are the five engagement life-cycle stages? | Risk assessment/internal inquiry; winning the work; contractual negotiation; delivery/project management; close-out/quality assurance. | Add scenario-specific actions under each stage, not only the stage names. |
| PISC-21 | What risk does early risk assessment control? | Accepting work the firm cannot safely, credibly, profitably, or ethically deliver. | Use for bid/no-bid questions. |
| PISC-22 | What risk does contractual negotiation control? | Later disagreement over scope, price, responsibilities, legal terms, assumptions, exclusions, change control, or sign-off. | Link weak contracts to delivery and close-out conflict. |
| PISC-23 | What is the exam move for engagement life-cycle questions? | Use each stage as a heading and add two scenario-specific activities or controls under each. | This fixes weak answers that only list the five stages. |
| PISC-24 | What should be checked in bid/no-bid judgement? | Capability, client fit, risk, time, profitability, mandatory criteria, reputation, reliance, and strategic value. | Use when asked whether the consulting firm should pursue the opportunity. |

## Communication And Advice Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PISC-25 | What is professional advice? | Structured judgement in response to a client problem, based on evidence, analysis, options, trade-offs, assumptions, limits, risks, recommendation, and next steps. | Do not give only facts. Turn facts into judgement and action. |
| PISC-26 | Information vs analysis vs advice? | Information is facts or observations; analysis explains what the facts mean; advice says what the client should do. | Use when the scenario says the consultant gave information but no recommendation. |
| PISC-27 | Finding vs recommendation? | A finding states what was discovered; a recommendation states what should be done about it. | Use for report, presentation, and professional advice questions. |
| PISC-28 | What makes advice actionable? | It links objective, evidence, reasoning, options, trade-offs, assumptions, limits, risks, recommendation, and next steps. | Use this as the advice paragraph structure. |
| PISC-29 | What should a consulting report do? | Answer the client's question and support decisions, rather than record a diary of activities. | Use for report-writing critique questions. |
| PISC-30 | What are common report-writing mistakes? | Diary-style writing, patronising tone, excessive length, failing to re-check the brief, adding new material at the end, overly complex diagrams, and giving every thought instead of the reasoning needed. | Use when asked to improve a poor report. |
| PISC-31 | How should technical material be communicated to executives? | Translate accurately into business impact, evidence, risk, options, recommendation, and next steps without patronising the audience. | Use for audience-awareness scenarios. |

## Negotiation, Evidence, And Problem-Solving Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PISC-32 | What can be negotiated in consulting work? | Scope, price, timeline, deliverables, responsibilities, assumptions, resources, change requests, sign-off, workshops, travel, support, and implementation work. | Avoid vague "win-win" language; name the variable under pressure. |
| PISC-33 | What should a consultant know before negotiating? | Bottom line, walk-away point, negotiable and non-negotiable items, likely hard questions, objective criteria, and relationship impact. | Use for low price, fast timeline, or extra work scenarios. |
| PISC-34 | Meeting vs interview vs workshop? | Meeting: governance, decisions, progress, risk, alignment. Interview: depth, stakeholder view, hidden issues, assumptions. Workshop: shared understanding, collaboration, prioritisation, design, problem solving. | Choose the format based on evidence or decision need. |
| PISC-35 | What must a good meeting include? | Purpose, relevant participants, agenda, prepared materials, time discipline, visible actions, and prompt minutes or agreed actions. | Use when a scenario shows vague meetings or weak follow-up. |
| PISC-36 | What must a good interview include? | Open, closed, and probing questions; active listening; social cues; evidence trail; separation of observation, opinion, interpretation, and follow-up needs. | Use when evidence quality matters. |
| PISC-37 | What must a good workshop include? | Objective, activities, timing, participant plan, outputs, facilitation approach, and write-up. | Use when a scenario needs shared outputs or stakeholder alignment. |
| PISC-38 | What is OPQRST for meeting/interview planning? | Order/objectives, process, starting question, recording method, supplies/tools, timing. | Use as a fast agenda-planning recall cue. |
| PISC-39 | Structured vs unstructured problem? | Structured problems are routine with clear goals, rules, and sufficient information. Unstructured problems are ambiguous, contested, incomplete, and require judgement. | Use before recommending a problem-solving method. |
| PISC-40 | What are the IESE six steps? | Problem definition, criteria selection, alternative development, analysis, decision, action plan. | Apply each step to scenario facts. |
| PISC-41 | What is the main IESE exam trap? | Jumping to the client's preferred solution before defining the real problem and decision criteria. | If evidence is incomplete, recommend discovery, pilot, staged rollout, or decision gate. |
| PISC-42 | Tame vs wicked problem? | Tame problems can be solved or controlled through a defined method; wicked problems lack definitive formulation, simple stopping rules, or true-false solutions. | Use to explain ambiguity and judgement. |
| PISC-43 | What is hypothesis-driven consulting? | Starting with a provisional, testable answer, defining evidence that would support or reject it, gathering evidence, and updating the hypothesis. | Do not defend the first idea blindly. |
| PISC-44 | Hypothesis vs assumption? | A hypothesis is tested and updated; an assumption is a stated condition or dependency. | Useful for problem-solving and proposal questions. |

## Control, Risk, Conflict, And Close Cards

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PISC-45 | What is risk? | Uncertainty that may affect project or business objectives. | Identify the specific uncertainty, not just "the project may fail". |
| PISC-46 | What is governance? | Decision rights, ownership, reporting, escalation, approvals, roles, responsibilities, and sign-off authority. | Governance makes risks visible and manageable. |
| PISC-47 | What is the risk-register answer pattern? | Risk, evidence, consequence, mitigation/control, owner or escalation path. | Use for every risk question. |
| PISC-48 | What are the risk response options? | Avoid, mitigate/reduce, transfer, share, and accept. | Match the response to the scenario rather than listing generic mitigations. |
| PISC-49 | What is the governance weakness pattern from past papers? | Aspect needing governance, risk created by weak governance, governance control, and how the control reduces risk. | Use for governance subquestions. |
| PISC-50 | What is scope creep? | Informal expansion of work without agreed changes to scope, price, timing, resources, deliverables, or quality. | A friendly "yes" can still create delivery and relationship risk. |
| PISC-51 | What should happen when a client asks for extra work? | Assess cost, time, resources, deliverables, quality, assumptions, and contract impact; document through change control if agreed. | Do not treat it as informal goodwill. |
| PISC-52 | What are the three conflict types? | Intragroup conflict, intergroup conflict, and client-consultant conflict. | Classify the conflict and prove it with facts. |
| PISC-53 | What causes consulting conflict? | Work overload, deadlines, fear, poor communication, distrust, loss of control, weak sponsorship, scope ambiguity, and politics. | Diagnose the cause underneath behaviour. |
| PISC-54 | What is the conflict response pattern? | Separate person, behaviour, issue, and decision; prepare evidence; use neutral language; listen for interests; explain trade-offs; escalate through governance if needed. | Use when stakeholders resist or behave badly. |
| PISC-55 | When should escalation happen? | When the issue exceeds the consultant's authority or threatens delivery, scope, trust, quality, or client action. | Escalate with evidence through governance, not personal confrontation. |
| PISC-56 | What is close-out? | Formal completion that confirms scope has been met, unresolved matters are visible, ownership transfers, and the client knows what to do next. | Do not stop at "deliver the solution". |
| PISC-57 | What belongs in close-out? | Final presentation, QA review, final iterations, acceptance, sponsor sign-off, handover, support arrangements, residual risks, lessons learned, and governance reporting. | Use for engagement-ending questions. |
| PISC-58 | What are close-out report types to remember? | Analytical report, lessons learned report, follow-on action recommendations, ROI report, and end-of-project report. | If asked for reports, list named types first. |
| PISC-59 | Delivery completion vs close-out? | Delivery provides outputs; close-out confirms acceptance, sign-off, handover, residual risks, support, lessons learned, and transfer of ownership. | Use this distinction when a client disputes completion. |
| PISC-60 | What is the last-minute PISC checklist? | Name the framework, use scenario facts, include controls, separate proposal from report, add sign-off/assumptions/exclusions/ownership, and recommend a pilot or staged decision when evidence is incomplete. | Use before submitting any answer. |
