## Scope

This quiz tests recall of our own proposal and its sources, one pitch section at a time. Do one section per sitting, answer from memory, and check the proposal only after you finish.

Answer from the **submitted proposal** unless a question says otherwise. Questions marked *(Pivot)* matter only if the pitch moves to the delivery-pressure framing from the Week 06 notes. Decide the pitch framing before attempting the Pivot questions.

No answer key is included. Grade each section after you attempt it.

## How to Use

1. Pick one section (A to H) and answer without notes, aloud or in writing.
2. Give each answer a direct claim, the evidence, then the limit.
3. Mark your own confidence before checking (high, medium, low), so overconfident errors show up.
4. Grade against the proposal text or ask for marking.
5. Re-sit a section when you score below 80%.

## Section A: Problem and Motivation

1. State the practical problem the proposal addresses in two sentences.
2. What did Miller et al. (2025) report, and what is the design and limit of that evidence?
3. Why does the proposal say a finished change "does not reveal all the assumptions or reasoning behind it"?
4. The proposal says the shared time limit "represents a delivery constraint." What does it say the study will not do about deadlines?
5. Who would use the findings, and what decision would they inform?

## Section B: Literature and Research Gap

6. Kuutila et al. (2020) reviewed how many papers, and what general pattern did they find? What caution applies?
7. What does Austin's (2001) agency model suggest about why developers take shortcuts, and why is it not a direct test?
8. Match each study to its main finding:
   - Barke et al. (2023)
   - Chen et al. (2025)
   - Shen and Tamkin (2026)
   - Padmakumar et al. (2026)
9. Becker et al. (2025): give the participants, number of tasks, headline result, and the setting limit. Why did Becker et al. (2026) say newer estimates were unreliable?
10. Balepur et al. (2026): what did agent users achieve initially, what happened to comprehension, and why does the study not compare sustained agent use with manual work?
11. Borg et al. (2026): what two groups of developers were involved, what did they find, and why does excluding failed solutions limit the finding?
12. What did Jiang et al. (2026) find about late requirements, and why does rewriting alone not prove a worse result?
13. State the research gap in your own words. Which three studies does the proposal say do not answer it?
14. Why must the gap not be described as "nobody has studied AI and time pressure"? Name the closest study that already varies time limits.
15. What is the bounded search claim the proposal can honestly make, and what searches have not yet been done?

## Section C: Theory and Conceptual Framework

16. What distinction does Pennington (1987) make, and how does the proposal use it?
17. Does the proposal test Pennington's model? Explain the role of the interviews.
18. Name one limitation of applying Pennington to agent-assisted work.
19. Name two other frameworks from the working notes that could have been used (with source), and say why the proposal did not make them central.
20. How does the proposal separate understanding how code runs from understanding what business need it serves?

## Section D: Research Question and Aims

21. State the research question exactly.
22. State the aim in one sentence, including the time window and what both groups draw from.
23. The question says "quality", but the main data analysis measure is accepted tasks per developer. Explain how the proposal links the two, and where the mismatch could be challenged.
24. What would be a clearer wording of the question if the study primarily measures accepted work?
25. *(Pivot)* State the Week 06 delivery-pressure question and explain how it differs from the submitted question.

## Section E: Research Design

26. What kind of design is it, how many participants, how are they assigned, and what is balanced?
27. Which eligibility criteria apply, and what does the proposal say eligibility will *not* depend on?
28. What do participants do before timing starts, and why?
29. How long do both groups have, how many repositories are involved, and what does the proposal say about the planned "two versus eight tasks"?
30. How are tasks organised so that an earlier mistake cannot block a later task?
31. What is the difference between the manual and AI conditions in tools, documentation and AI setup?
32. What happens at the two-hour mark, and what does submission time record?
33. Why is switching between repositories part of the study rather than noise? Name one way it is also a confound.
34. *(Pivot)* In the Week 06 design, what is manipulated, what stays constant, and why record agent latency and number of runs?

## Section F: Measures and Analysis

35. What is the main productivity measure?
36. Describe the three components the end-to-end tests cover and name the benchmark whose distinction they follow.
37. Who else reviews submissions, and what must be true for a change to be accepted?
38. Why are reviewers kept from seeing group labels "where feasible", and why is perfect blinding unlikely?
39. How does the proposal handle developers with no submissions?
40. What does the observer record, and when are recordings used?
41. What are the interviews about, and what does the proposal say it will not do with them?
42. How does the analysis treat differences between what participants say and what they did?
43. Which claim does the proposal explicitly avoid making about a defect and understanding?
44. Name two measurement weaknesses an audience could press on (for example reviewer agreement) and say how you would respond.

## Section G: Ethics, Feasibility and Limits

45. List the consent and withdrawal commitments, including what must be explained about AI providers.
46. How are code, recordings, transcripts and consent records stored, and how are participants identified in analysis?
47. Why does recruitment take place outside the researcher's workplace?
48. What preparation work does the study require, and how many developer-hours do sessions need?
49. Which limits does the proposal state about sample size, repositories, AI setup and recruitment?
50. What does the proposal say it will *not* assess or justify?
51. There is a sentence in the limitations that mentions a three- to fourfold productivity gain. Is it supported by a reference in the proposal? What should you do before the pitch?

## Section H: Contribution and Implications

52. What would the study show if submissions rise but accepted work does not?
53. What would it support if accepted work rises, and what bound does the proposal put on that claim?
54. How could developers' accounts add to the outcome data?
55. Which outcomes will be reported even if unwelcome?
56. What type of contribution is this in Gregor's (2006) terms, and what would be needed to claim more?

## Section I: Appendices and AI Use

57. What is in Appendix A and Appendix B?
58. What did the AI literature review do well, according to the comparison?
59. Name the weakness the comparison identifies in the AI review's answer about ambiguous requirements.
60. How would you explain, in a pitch Q&A, that an AI model's output was compared against your own review rather than used as the review?

## Section J: Integrated Written Answers (Exam-Style)

61. In about 90 seconds, give the problem, gap, question and method, with no notes.
62. Defend the sample size and design in four sentences, stating at least one limit.
63. Critique this answer: "Our study proves that AI agents make developers more productive because accepted tasks will rise." Rewrite it so it fits the proposal.
64. A researcher says, "Becker already answered this, so your study adds nothing." Respond in under 45 seconds.
65. Pick one limitation and explain how it would change what you claim from the results.

## Grading Notes

- Mark note-based answers `0.0` to `1.0` (`1.0` correct and complete, `0.75` mostly correct, `0.5` partly correct, `0.25` minimal, `0.0` incorrect, blank or too vague).
- Create a short rubric per question before marking.
- Score each section separately, then record the overall percentage.
- Do not credit a study match, number or finding that is not in the proposal or sources.
- Reward answers that state the limit, and mark down any answer claiming "first", "proves" or "causes".
- Re-sit a section below 80% with new questions that continue the numbering from Q66.
- Questions marked *(Pivot)* are excluded from the score unless the pitch uses the delivery-pressure framing.
