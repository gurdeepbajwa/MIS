## What the Course Requires

The course does not expect students to read or memorise every article. The task is to:

1. Read Gregor (2006) at the start of Week 2.
2. After Week 2 Tutorial Question 6, choose at least two clusters.
3. Browse an abstract from each possible cluster before choosing.
4. Read the papers that seem useful between Weeks 2 and 4.
5. Focus on what each theory seeks to explain and how it explains it.

## Core Reading

Gregor, S. (2006). The nature of theory in information systems. *MIS Quarterly, 30*(3), 611–642. https://doi.org/10.2307/25148742

## Cluster 1: Activity and Boundary-Spanning Practice

- **Activity theory:** Karanasios, S., & Allen, D. (2018). Activity theory in information systems research. *Information Systems Journal, 28*, 439–441. https://doi.org/10.1111/isj.12184
- **Boundary spanning:** Levina, N., & Vaast, E. (2005). The emergence of boundary spanning competence in practice: Implications for implementation and use of information systems. *MIS Quarterly, 29*(2), 335–363. https://www.jstor.org/stable/25148682

## Cluster 2: Business Process Analysis

- **Process Mining Manifesto:** van der Aalst, W., et al. (2012). Process mining manifesto. In F. Daniel, K. Barkaoui, & S. Dustdar (Eds.), *Business process management workshops* (Lecture Notes in Business Information Processing, Vol. 99). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-28108-2_19
- **Business process querying framework:** Polyvyanyy, A., Ouyang, C., Barros, A., & van der Aalst, W. M. P. (2017). Process querying: Enabling business intelligence through query-based process analytics. *Decision Support Systems, 100*, 41–56. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dss.2017.04.011

## Cluster 3: Resources and Competitive Advantage

- **Resource-Based View:** Wade, M., & Hulland, J. (2004). Review: The resource-based view and information systems research: Review, extension, and suggestions for future research. *MIS Quarterly, 28*(1), 107–142. https://doi.org/10.2307/25148626
- **Resource orchestration model:** Sirmon, D. G., Hitt, M. A., Ireland, R. D., & Gilbert, B. A. (2011). Resource orchestration to create competitive advantage: Breadth, depth, and life cycle effects. *Journal of Management, 37*(5), 1390–1412. https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206310385695

## Cluster 4: Organisations, Power, and Structure

- **Agency theory:** Panda, B., & Leepsa, N. M. (2017). Agency theory: Review of theory and evidence on problems and perspectives. *Indian Journal of Corporate Governance, 10*(1), 74–95. https://doi.org/10.1177/0974686217701467
- **Structuration theory:** Jones, M. R., & Karsten, H. (2008). Giddens's structuration theory and information systems research. *MIS Quarterly, 32*(1), 127–157. https://doi.org/10.2307/25148831

## Cluster 5: Information and Digital Experience

- **Information behaviour models:** Robson, A., & Robinson, L. (2013). Building on models of information behaviour: Linking information seeking and communication. *Journal of Documentation, 69*(2), 169–193. https://doi.org/10.1108/00220411311300039
- **Digital journeys:** Chang, S., & Gomes, C. (2017). Digital journeys: A perspective on understanding the digital experiences of international students. *Journal of International Students, 7*(2), 347–366. https://doi.org/10.32674/jis.v7i2.385

## Cluster 6: Behaviour and Technology Uptake

- **Theory of Planned Behaviour:** Ajzen, I. (1991). The theory of planned behavior. *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 50*(2), 179–211. https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(91)90020-T
- **Brief update:** Bosnjak, M., Ajzen, I., & Schmidt, P. (2020). The theory of planned behavior: Selected recent advances and applications. *Europe's Journal of Psychology, 16*(3), 352–356. https://doi.org/10.5964/ejop.v16i3.3107
- **Decomposed Theory of Planned Behaviour:** Taylor, S., & Todd, P. (1995). Decomposition and crossover effects in the theory of planned behavior: A study of consumer adoption intentions. *International Journal of Research in Marketing, 12*(2), 137–155. https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-8116(94)00019-K

## Cluster 7: Human–Computer Interaction and Design

- **Human–computer interaction contributions:** Wobbrock, J. O., & Kientz, J. A. (2016). Research contributions in human-computer interaction. *Interactions, 23*(3), 38–44. https://doi.org/10.1145/2907069
- **Research through design:** Stappers, P. J., & Giaccardi, E. (2013). Research through design. In M. Soegaard & R. F. Dam (Eds.), *The encyclopedia of human-computer interaction* (2nd ed.). Interaction Design Foundation. https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/book/the-encyclopedia-of-human-computer-interaction-2nd-ed/research-through-design
- Deep dive: [[Week 04 - Cluster 7 Design and Interaction Deep Dive]]

## Selection Record

- Chosen cluster 1: Cluster 2 — Business Process Analysis
- Chosen readings: *Process Mining Manifesto* and *Process Querying*
- Reason for choosing it: Shows how IS research can use process data and design frameworks to analyse and improve organisational work.
- Chosen cluster 2: Cluster 5 — Information and Digital Experience
- Chosen readings: *Building on Models of Information Behaviour* and *Digital Journeys*
- Reason for choosing it: Shows how explanatory models can connect information seeking, communication, digital sources, and cross-border experience.
