## Opening Line Format

> My research question relates to **`<context>`**, and so **`<question>`**. It further talks about **`<more context>`**.

The pattern puts the context in the first sentence and makes the question follow from it ("and so"). The closing "it further talks about" clause adds the second layer of context (the extra factors or setting the study also covers) and previews what the next slides will unpack. It works as both the first spoken line and the cue for the context slides that follow.

### Draft "Further" Clauses (Edit Before Use)

Drawn from our own documents. Pick one or two, not all.

- **Submitted framing:** "It further talks about working across several repositories within a shared two-hour limit, and about how developers reason about the changes they make." (Proposal: four repositories, two hours, observation and interviews.)
- **Delivery-pressure framing:** "It further talks about whether a shorter time allowance changes what developers understand, check and preserve when business rules change." (Week 06 notes: new, obsolete and unchanged rules; process measures secondary.)
- **Either framing:** "It further talks about delivered work that passes quality checks, not just the number of submissions." (Proposal introduction: findings should set workload expectations on work that passes quality checks.)

### Draft Openers (Edit Before Use)

These use only wording and claims from our own documents. Read each aloud and shorten it to what you would actually say.

**Submitted framing (AI vs no AI):**

> My research question relates to developers who must apply business changes in repositories they don't know, now with AI coding agents available, and so I ask: how does the quality of code changes made to meet business requirements differ between developers working with and without an AI coding agent?

**Delivery-pressure framing (Week 06 notes):**

> My research question relates to developers using AI coding agents to change unfamiliar repositories under delivery pressure, and so I ask: how does delivery pressure affect developers' ability to implement business requirement changes in an unfamiliar repository using an AI coding agent?

### Checks

- Is the context short enough to say in one breath (about 15 to 25 words)?
- Does the "further" clause add one new layer only, so the opener stays under about 20 seconds spoken?
- Does the context name the situation the audience will see on the next slides, without new terms?
- Does "and so" hold? The question should be a direct consequence of the context, not a separate topic.
- If you use the question slide later (steps 1 to 6 in the sequence above), keep this line as the spoken bridge between the gap slide and the question slide.

## What the Feedback Implies

An audience of university researchers will not necessarily share our starting picture: what a coding agent is, why an unfamiliar repository matters, or why delivered work and submission counts differ. If the question arrives first, they must hold its terms without the situation that makes it matter. Put the situation first so the question reads as the natural next step.

## Decisions to Make

1. **Which question appears on the question slide?** The submitted proposal compares AI with no AI. The Week 06 notes settled on delivery pressure. Steps 2 and 5 change depending on this.
2. **How much literature on the context slides?** The research-synthesis marks are heavily weighted in the proposal rubric (40 of 100), but the pitch brief may weight things differently. Check the brief.
3. **Does the question stay verbatim?** Keep wording consistent with the proposal unless you intentionally revise it. Revisions should be disclosed and explained, not silent.
4. **Timing.** Allocate time once the pitch length is known. Keep context to roughly the first quarter, so the method and limits are not squeezed.
