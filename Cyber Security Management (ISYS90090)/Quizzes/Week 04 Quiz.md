## Quiz 1

### Note-Based Questions

1. Which option correctly distinguishes a threat, threat actor, attack vector, and attack?
   - A. A threat is a weakness, the actor is the asset, the vector is the damage, and the attack is the control.
   - B. A threat is the harmful action, the actor is the route used, the vector is the attacker, and the attack is the possible danger.
   - C. A threat is any loss of confidentiality, the actor is the system owner, the vector is the vulnerability, and the attack is the business impact.
   - D. A threat is an ongoing danger, the actor causes the impact, the vector is the route used, and the attack exploits a weakness to cause harm.

2. Which statement correctly distinguishes an organisation's risk profile from its risk appetite?
   - A. Risk profile is the security budget; risk appetite is the number of controls installed.
   - B. Risk profile is the full set of identified risk scenarios; risk appetite is the amount of risk the organisation accepts.
   - C. Risk profile is one successful attack; risk appetite is the damage caused by it.
   - D. Risk profile is the risk accepted after controls; risk appetite is every threat facing the organisation.

3. A trusted partner later develops a competing product and seeks knowledge outside its stated need. What is the best management response?
   - A. Keep the same access because the partner passed the original checks.
   - B. Ask IT to install stronger software without reviewing the relationship.
   - C. Monitor the partner's motives, behaviour, and knowledge access, then restrict access when warning signs show it may now act as a competitor.
   - D. End all business partnerships because trusted parties always become threats.

4. Which response combines formal, informal, and technological controls to stop staff sending confidential files to personal email accounts?
   - A. Publish a policy and apply penalties, without training or technical checks.
   - B. Set a formal handling policy, train staff, and use an email control that blocks and logs prohibited transfers.
   - C. Tell staff to be careful and ask managers to watch them.
   - D. Install an email filter without defining protected information or teaching staff how to handle it.

5. A supplier accidentally emails confidential prices to a rival, which knowingly uses them to underbid the intended company. Which analysis is most accurate?
   - A. Both actions are software attacks because email carried the information.
   - B. The supplier committed sabotage, while the rival made a human error.
   - C. The supplier's action is human error; the rival's deliberate use may form part of competitive espionage.
   - D. The whole event is technological obsolescence because email is an old system.

6. A criminal steals an employee's valid login details and uses them to access internal files. How should the organisation classify the actor?
   - A. A credentialed external actor creating an insider threat.
   - B. A negligent insider because the account belongs to an employee.
   - C. A malicious employee because all valid accounts represent staff.
   - D. A software failure because the system accepted the login.

7. A company buys a third-party payroll application that may contain serious flaws. Information security must create a safe environment that protects both the application and the company from those flaws. Which organisational role does this describe?
   - A. Protect data, information, and knowledge.
   - B. Protect the organisation's ability to function.
   - C. Safeguard technology assets.
   - D. Enable the safe operation of applications on IT systems.

8. One attack team withdraws after the company raises its defence costs, but new teams continue targeting the same asset with similar methods. What is the strongest inference?
   - A. The incidents must result from unrelated staff mistakes.
   - B. The controls worked, so the company can stop investigating the threat.
   - C. A persistent sponsor may have a strategic motive and may keep replacing the operational attack teams.
   - D. The asset has no value because the first team stopped.

9. Which statement is an insight rather than a fact from the Horizon case?
   - A. Emil copied hundreds of NextGen files before leaving Horizon.
   - B. Attackers previously spent time inside Horizon's finance network.
   - C. Visitors asked detailed technical questions and took photographs.
   - D. Horizon lacked joined security ownership and failed to connect separate warnings into one long-term threat picture.

10. Which is the strongest and most specific risk description?
    - A. Cybercriminals may attack the construction company and cause problems.
    - B. A rival may recruit an authorised estimator to export the confidential bill of quantities through valid access, exposing supply costs and allowing the rival to underbid the company.
    - C. The bill of quantities is risky because competitors want financial information.
    - D. The company should install MFA and improve cybersecurity awareness.
