# Cyber Flashcard Deck

Course: [[Cyber Security Management (ISYS90090)/Course Overview|Cyber Security Management (ISYS90090)]]
Purpose: active recall for the multiple-choice, short-answer, and Section C parts of the exam.
Sources: [[Cyber Exam Cheat Sheet]], [[Cyber Exam Study Guide - Section C]], [[Horizon Question Workbook - Facts and Insights]]

## How to Use

Cover the `Back` and `Exam use` columns. Say the answer aloud in 20–40 seconds, then check that you used the course term and explained the reason, not only the label.

Marking code:

- `Hard`: could not recall the concept or distinction.
- `Partial`: remembered the idea but missed course wording or the application.
- `Ready`: can answer clearly and apply it to a case.

Wave plan: do the **Section C Spine** and **Case Skills** first, then one week at a time. Return after a day or two for delayed recall. Shuffle the order before the exam so sequence does not cue the answers.

## Section C Spine

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CYB-01 | What are the three Section C questions and their marks? | C1 five key insights (15); C2 single most critical risk using the triplet (10); C3 five recommendations (25). | Know this before you read the case. |
| CYB-02 | What is the exam format? | About 20 multiple choice, a short-answer section of about 30%, and a case section worth 50%. | Spend less time on the easier sections and protect time for the case. |
| CYB-03 | Fact versus insight? | A fact is written in the case. An insight is a root cause inferred by linking several facts. | Never present a restated fact as an insight. |
| CYB-04 | How do you write one insight? | State two or more facts, name the root cause they show, give the reasoning, then say why it matters. | Three marks per insight. |
| CYB-05 | What does "check the altitude" mean? | Place each answer on the strategic, tactical, or operational level. Strategic covers organisation design, ownership, and why teams do not talk. | Operational-only answers lose marks. |
| CYB-06 | C2 template? | Asset; threat actor; one specific vulnerability; impact; and why it beats the rejected risks. | One risk only. |
| CYB-07 | What three questions test each recommendation? | What changes? How does that reduce the risk? Who owns it? | Weak answers name a control and stop. |
| CYB-08 | What rule does the tutor run sheet enforce? | Each recommendation must say how it reduces the risk named in C2. | A product list earns very little. |
| CYB-09 | What spread should the five recommendations have? | Several altitudes and several types: formal, technical, informal. | Avoid five technical operational fixes. |
| CYB-10 | What are common insight root causes? | Wrong asset; no owner; silos; controls for the wrong adversary; formal management without effect; people route beats technology; weak detection and response; compliance mistaken for security. | Self-check, then prove each with case facts. |

## Case Skills

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |

| CYB-11 | What turns "IT" into a usable asset? | Name the exact service, information, or knowledge, and why it has value. | "IT" is too broad. |

| CYB-18 | Why are short- and long-term costs different? | Short term is visible and one-off. Long term is a changed market, such as a lasting competitor. | State both in impact answers. |

## Week 2 — Triplet, CIA, Roles, CISO

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CYB-19 | What is the triplet? | Information resources, threats, and controls. A vulnerability lets the threat harm the asset. | Start every case from this. |
| CYB-20 | What is a risk scenario? | A specific threat exploiting a specific vulnerability in a specific asset to cause an impact. | Each combination is a separate risk. |
| CYB-21 | Risk profile versus risk appetite? | Profile is all risk scenarios together. Appetite is how much risk the organisation will accept. | Mark scenarios against appetite. |
| CYB-22 | Three asset categories? | Digital platforms; data and information; knowledge. | Knowledge is explicit or tacit. |
| CYB-23 | Explicit versus tacit knowledge? | Explicit can be written or recorded. Tacit is experience and judgement held in a person. | Tacit knowledge walks out with people. |
| CYB-24 | Define C, I, and A. | Confidentiality: read only by authorised parties. Integrity: changed only by authorised parties. Availability: usable when needed. | Give a database example for each. |
| CYB-25 | Three control categories? | Formal (prescriptive), informal (suggestive), technological (restrictive). | They must work together. |
| CYB-26 | Four roles of information security? | Protect ability to function; enable safe operation of applications; protect data, information, and knowledge; safeguard technology assets. | IT security covers only the fourth. |
| CYB-27 | Why does CISO reporting line matter? | It affects access to decisions, funds, independence, and board attention. | Only 8% reported to the CEO in the survey. |

## Week 5 — Defence in Depth

| Card | Front | Back | Exam use |
