## Draft Analysis Frame

For each case, identify:

1. The sequence of events rather than one isolated incident.
2. The exact assets and why they matter.
3. The threat actors, sponsors, motives, and likely commitment.
4. The vulnerabilities and attack vectors used.
5. The actors' tactics, techniques, and procedures.
6. The effects on confidentiality, integrity, availability, and the business.
7. The control and management gaps that allowed the events to continue.
8. Controls that match the exact risk rather than a broad list of tools.

## Facts, Symptoms, and Insights

- A **fact** is evidence stated or shown in the case. It often describes a visible symptom.
- An **insight** is the underlying problem, pattern, or root cause inferred from one or more facts.
- Do not present a restated fact as the case diagnosis.
- Link several facts, explain what they reveal, and state why the inferred cause best explains the events.
- Recommend action against the cause, while also containing any urgent symptoms.
- **Exam check:** If the case directly writes or shows the point, treat it as a fact, not an insight. The insight must be an inference supported by facts.

Use this structure:

> The case states **[facts]**. Together, these facts indicate **[insight or root cause]** because **[reasoning]**. This caused or enabled **[impact]**. The organisation should therefore **[cause-focused response]**.
