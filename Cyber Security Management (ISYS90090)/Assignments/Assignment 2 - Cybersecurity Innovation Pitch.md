## Choose One Problem Area

1. **Threat actor response:** improve how firms find, stop, or respond to insider threats, state-backed actors, cybercrime groups, hacktivists, or another threat actor.
2. **Asset protection:** improve how firms protect key assets such as intellectual property, private information, or digital platforms.
3. **CISO effectiveness:** create a tool, method, or framework that helps a CISO carry out the role.
4. **Risk communication:** improve how firms explain cyber risk to non-technical executives, staff, customers, or partners.
5. **Incident readiness and response:** improve how firms prepare for, find, or respond to cyber incidents.

## Solution Types

The proposed solution may focus on one or more of these forms:

- a new process, method, or framework;
- a new use of current or new technology;
- a new structure, policy, or procedure; or
- a response to staff behaviour or workplace culture.

## Required Analysis

- Give industry examples and proof that the problem matters.
- Use academic or industry work to support the proposed response.
- Apply the cybersecurity triplet: asset, threat, risk, and control.
- Account for the CISO's view and real limits within a firm.
- Show sound use of two or three cybersecurity management ideas from the course.
- Address one main stakeholder's view.
- Explain how the proposal improves current practice and can work within real limits.

The lecturer stated that a broad or narrow problem has no built-in marking advantage. The team must make a strong case that fits the available time, evidence, and proposed response.

### Diagnose Before Recommending

The tutor stressed that the group must prove that it understands the problem before presenting a solution:

1. State the symptoms or observed evidence.
2. Infer and explain the underlying problem or root cause.
3. Show the business and security impact.
4. Propose an innovation that addresses that cause.
5. Link each recommendation or control to a stated part of the problem.

If the group cannot define the problem clearly, its solution will appear unsupported or aimed at the wrong issue. This directly supports the rubric's 25 marks for problem definition and significance.

## Source Rules

Use at least five sound sources:

- at least two journal articles or conference papers;
- at least two reports, case studies, or expert sources from respected firms, government bodies, or research groups; and
- current examples that show why the problem matters.

For fast-changing topics, favour sources from the last three to five years. Use one citation style in a steady way. The course suggests SciMago rankings when judging journal quality.

The Week 3 lecture restated the minimum of two academic and two industry sources and said the teaching team would check the sources shown in the submitted slides.

## Lecturer's Knowledge-Map Example

The lecturer gave a sample pitch to show the expected logic. It is an example of the method, not a topic that teams must copy.

- **Problem:** Firms grant partners access to systems without knowing all the information and knowledge those systems expose.
- **Asset:** Business knowledge held across systems and teams.
- **Threat actor:** A partner, supplier, contractor, or competitor that misuses valid access.
- **Control idea:** Build a knowledge map that records where key knowledge sits and who should access it.
- **Process:** Use the map when granting access, review access and behaviour every three to six months, and trigger an alert when a party reaches knowledge outside its stated need.
- **Business use:** Review or end access when a partner's behaviour begins to match that of a competitor.

The example shows the required chain from a real problem, through the triplet model, to a workable control and delivery process.

## High-Mark Check

- The problem is narrow, current, and tied to a clear business harm.
- Evidence proves the size and value of the problem; it does not just describe it.
- The response adds a clear new feature or method over current practice.
- The team deals with cost, staff, time, policy, and adoption limits.
- Course ideas support the pitch instead of appearing as a list of terms.
- The triplet model names the asset, threat, weakness or risk link, and matching controls.
- The team explains the CISO or other main stakeholder's needs and choices.
- Each speaker stays within time and can answer questions about the whole pitch.
