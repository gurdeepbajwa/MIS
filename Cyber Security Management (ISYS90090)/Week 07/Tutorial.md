## Workshop Task

Diagnose why Horizon's policy has little effect. The task asks for **three faults**, then **one priority fix**, within one revision cycle, one budget cycle and the current staff.

Each fault needs three parts:

1. What Karim found: a fact from the case.
2. What it costs Horizon: the likely business consequence, with reasoning.
3. The relevant lecture condition or policy-quality attribute.

Choose which fault to fix first and explain why the other faults can wait. The deck allocates 10 minutes to faults, seven to the priority and seven to reports within a 30-minute exercise. Three groups report for 90 seconds each; others challenge their reasoning. The output is a diagnosis and choice of fix, not a new policy draft.

## Facts Versus Insights

Facts include the policy being held by Kurt, its technical and long-winded wording, and its age of three years. An insight must explain a underlying problem supported by these facts, such as a failure to make policy usable across the business.

Do not treat an inferred consequence as a proven event. For example, poor distribution supports concern about compliance and accountability; it does not prove the exact outcome of a future employment dispute. The deck's dismissal example is a teaching claim, not a verified legal conclusion.

One slide says Karim never reads a policy statement, while the supplied original case explicitly describes him reading and inspecting wording. Use the original case as the evidence when analysing what he did.
