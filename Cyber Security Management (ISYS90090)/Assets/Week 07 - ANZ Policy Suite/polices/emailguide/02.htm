E-mail Content

2.1 Usage

E-mail is a preferred means of exchanging routine administrative and technical information, other informal correspondence and where appropriate, working drafts.

E-mail shall not be used as an alternate to signed documentation recording significant decisions or approvals or which otherwise warrants becoming part of ANZ's permanent record. Nor should it be used where other communication methods such as telephone or face to face meetings would be more effective.

Scanned signatures, and signature files containing graphics, shall never be sent by e-mail. This is because it is insecure and creates an unnecessary burden to the existing system resources.

It is essential that anyone using ANZ's e-mail system exercises prudent behaviour and ensures that they do not expose ANZ to the risk of litigation or damage to its reputation.

All messages created, sent, received or stored on the ANZ e-mail system are and will remain the property of ANZ.

2.2 Use of Non-approved E-mail Software

Use of non-approved e-mail products for sending, receiving or storing e-mail, including Internet based products and access to such Internet sites supporting these products (eg: Hotmail, PopMail, Yahoo! Mail, etc.) is not permitted.

2.3 Unacceptable Content and Usage

ANZ considers certain uses of the e-mail system as unacceptable and not permitted. Messages must not contain content that may reasonably be considered offensive or defamatory to any other person. Offensive content would include, but is not limited to sexual comments or images including any type of pornographic material, racial slurs, gender-specific comments or any comments that would offend someone on the basis of his or her age, gender, sexual orientation, religious or political beliefs, national origin or disability.

  • Spamming
    ANZ's e-mail system shall not be used for spamming (i.e. sending of bulk junk e-mail) and broadcast messages, including general bulk e-mailing, without a specific and defined business purpose. Spamming is defined as the sending of bulk amounts of unsolicited messages, commonly referred to as junk e-mail. The sending of bulk junk e-mail can cause significant degradation to ANZ's e-mail network.
  • Spoofing
    ANZ's e-mail system shall not be used for spoofing purposes. Spoofing is defined as sending e-mail using fraudulent e-mail settings, or deceiving for the purpose of gaining access to someone else's resources. On the surface, a spoofed message appears to come from an authentic or authoritative e-mail account, but in reality does not. Spoofed e-mail is often connected with Internet hoaxes, individual pranks, and attempts to deceive recipients into divulging computer account passwords or other sensitive information. Spoofing of any kind is considered unacceptable and shall not be permitted.

2.4 Security

E-mail, especially that transmitted on the Internet, is not secure. The privacy and integrity of information cannot be guaranteed without specific controls.

2.5 "Uploading" and "Downloading" Software Via Internet E-mail

It is not permitted to upload or download software using Internet e-mail.

2.6 Procedures for Securing E-mail

ANZ's e-mail system shall not be used to transmit "Highly Restricted" or "Confidential" information, without the use of GIS approved products for securing that information (i.e. products that provide encryption and authentication). Information regarding authentication and encryption facilities for e-mail is available from GIS.

In such an instance where a product cannot satisfactorily secure such information, GIS will advise on acceptable compensating controls.

Use of unauthorised encryption schemes/mechanisms for the transfer of e-mail is not permitted.

2.7 Copyrighted Material and Intellectual Property

Much of the material existing on the Internet is copyrighted or is legally protected and is restricted in use without the permission of the owner of the copyright or legal protection. The Internet provides very easy mechanisms to copy this material and exposes ANZ to the potentially severe legal penalties stemming from improper use.

It is not permitted to transmit any information, including but not limited to e-mail, files, documents, graphic files, sound files, news-group messages, WEB pages, applications software, operating system software, or any other intellectual property protected by copyright or patent law if permission has not been expressly and officially provided.

2.8 Malicious Software

E-mail received from external e-mail services is a prolific source of viruses that can cause harm to ANZ or inadvertently be passed on to third parties.

Viruses are distributed as executable software or, as is increasingly common, embedded as a "macro" in documents and spreadsheets.

ANZ has a strict policy that all workstations are equipped with virus protection mechanisms and that the protection mechanisms are kept as up to date as possible.

2.9 E-mail Etiquette

Users of ANZ's e-mail system should always bear in mind that certain minimum standards of behaviour and usage will ensure that the e-mail system remains a valuable communications tool.

  • The following will assist users in their approach to e-mail:
  • Refrain from using ALL CAPITALS. It is difficult to read and is considered to be SHOUTING at the readers;
  • "Flame" mail is e-mail written in an angry tone - to avoid this, write your e-mail as if you were speaking to the person in front of them. "Be aware of the turn of phrase that you send, lest you engender anger in place of effective communications";
  • Be careful in the selection of recipients especially when using Global Address List, to ensure that messages are transmitted only to relevant recipients;
  • Forwarding of e-mail should generally be done only with the knowledge of the sender;
  • Incoming messages and attachments should only be included in replies or forwarded messages where necessary and when it does not present a risk to ANZ. They should not be included as a matter of routine;
  • Do not include very short documents as attachments.It is preferable to cut and paste the information from the short document into the body of the e-mail. This saves time and storage space for the recipient.
