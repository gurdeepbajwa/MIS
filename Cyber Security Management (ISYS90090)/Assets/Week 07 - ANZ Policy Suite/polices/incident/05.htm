Incidents

5.1 Breach

5.1.1
A breach is the intentional non-compliance of any ANZ Policy and the commission of a criminal offence. A criminal offence is a breach of the law. The law is any Act of Parliament or Common Law, as may be applicable within your geographical jurisdiction. An Act of Parliament is written law, such as the Crimes Act, whereas Common Law is unwritten law established through long standing judge made law (case law precedent).

Incident Example
You introduce malicious software (virus) via a diskette		  
 

 

Consequences

 
  • Destruction of information stored on ANZ assets
  • Record of policy breach and written warning added to your HR file
  • Criminal prosecution against you for criminal damage
  • Civil proceedings against you to recover cost of damage resulting from your action

 

  
 

 

Justification

  

 

  • Malicious software (viruses) can cause the temporary loss of a system making ANZ unable to trade
  • Cost of repairing the damaged computer and/or systems
  • Possible criminal prosecution and/or civil action against the ANZ by an affected third party
  • Unfavourable reports in the media

5.2 Violation

5.2.1
A violation is the non-compliance of any ANZ Policy, irrespective of whether it was intentional or not. Such non-compliance must be confirmed through a preliminary investigation process.

Incident Example
 You use ANZ equipment for your personal use whilst on ANZ premises e.g. E-mailing copyright software

  
 

 

Consequences

 
  • Record of policy violation and written warning added to your HR file
  • Possible criminal prosecution against you for obtaining property or financial advantage by deception

 

  
 

 

Justification

  

 

  • Loss of competitive advantage
  • Unfavourable reports in the media
  • Costs in controlling adverse media attention
  • Loss of customers
  • Possible civil action against the ANZ by the software vendor

5.3 Weakness

5.3.1
A weakness is a potential or suspected breach or violation of any ANZ Policy. The term 'potential' refers to the identification of added risk of non-compliance, whereas the term 'suspected' refers to possible or probable non-compliance with any ANZ Policy.

Incident Example
 Electronically detected unusual activity e.g. High number of unsuccessful password attempts

  
 

 

Consequences

 
  • There are no consequences unless the incident becomes a violation or breach

 

  
 

 

Justification

  

 

  • Possible attempted fraudulent activity
  • Unauthorised exposure to internal systems
  • Unauthorised access to private information
  
       

 

Incident Example
 Allowing an unauthorised person to enter a secured area, by not ensuring the door locks behind you

  
 

 

Consequences

 
  • There are no consequences unless the incident becomes a violation or breach

 

  
 

 

Justification

  

 

  • Possible attempted fraudulent activity
  • Unauthorised exposure to internal systems
  • Unauthorised access to private information
