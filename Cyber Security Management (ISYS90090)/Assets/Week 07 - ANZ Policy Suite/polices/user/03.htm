Your Security Principles

3.1 Information Classification

3.1.1
All information must be classified according to its sensitivity. The Creator or Owner of the information is responsible for assigning the classification.

Classification   Use  
  Highly Restricted   This classification applies to the most sensitive private information, which, if compromised, poses a major risk, and may cause serious damage and adversely affect ANZ. e.g. Passwords, cryptographic keys, board papers and payments.  
  Confidential   This classification applies to less sensitive private information, which, if compromised, poses a significant risk and/or may adversely affect ANZ e.g. Customer details, payroll details, and personnel details.  
  Internal   This classification applies to all other private information, which may pose, if compromised, a risk to ANZ. e.g. Internal publications and policies.  
  Public   For information approved for public release by an authorising manager. e.g. Authorised press releases and approved marketing literature.  
       

 

Classification allows all staff to determine what level of protection is needed for that information, and how it should be handled.

3.2 Business Use

Equipment and information provided to you remain the property of ANZ at all times.

ANZ Information Technology hardware, software and communications infrastructure (assets) shall be used for business purposes, within ANZ premises. Staff shall not download, store, use or transmit information, content or behaviour of the following nature:

  • offensive, including pornographic material
  • discriminatory on the basis of race, gender, colour, national origin, age, marital status, sex, political affiliation, religion, disability or sexual orientation
  • threatening or violent
  • slanderous or defamatory
  • harassment or promoting harassment
  • destructive (e.g. the distribution of malicious software)
  • personal gain including the conducting of outside business activities
  • any illegal activity
  • political or religious beliefs
  • spoofing (misrepresenting, obscuring, suppressing, or replacing a user's identity on the Internet)
  • fundraising, or charitable not sponsored by ANZ
  • gambling

3.3 Passwords

Your password (or passphrase) must always be at least 7 characters long and shall not be a single dictionary word, name, phone number, memorable date or your car licence plate number. It should also contain a mixture of letters (upper and lower case), numbers and special characters (like * and &). The bigger the range and less repetitive your password characters, the harder it is to 'crack' or guess it.

You will be able to guess at your password 3 times before the system freezes. This is to deter unauthorised users. To un-freeze, call Tech Assist or your local helpdesk.

Passwords issued from a privileged user are only valid for the first time of logging-on. Once you have logged on with this password you must change this password before being able to access the system.

Passwords must be changed a minimum of every 30 days and must not be reused for at least the next thirteen (13) consecutive changes.

DO NOT write your password down or display it anywhere. Remember your password and choose a password that is memorable to you, but hard to guess.

If you think someone else knows your password, change it immediately. If you think someone has been using or trying to use your ID and password, change your password and inform Tech Assist or your local helpdesk. All work done under your ID is your responsibility. You are also responsible for all e-mail sent and Internet sites visited by your ID.

3.4 Malicious Software

You must completely log off from your PC at least once every day to ensure that you have the latest version of the virus software.

You must check all diskettes and e-mail attachments for viruses before you use them in ANZ assets. You need to use the virus protection software that is supplied with your PC. Do not disable the anti-virus software.

Viruses can cause major problems. Some viruses produce amusing outcomes, but even this can affect the speed of your machine. In worse cases, viruses could infect critical systems in ANZ.

3.5 Continuity of Business

You are responsible for the backup of your local hard drive(s). Drives from F:\ onwards are network drives, which are backed up by IT Service Delivery. Please contact Tech Assist or local helpdesk for advice on how to do this. If you have a laptop remember to backup the hard drive(s) regularly, as it is at greater risk of loss.

When moving your laptop or other portable assets, ensure they are safe at all times, by either having them with you or securing them. Do not leave them unattended:

  • in cars or taxis
  • in cafes or restaurants
  • on public transport
  • at home (unless it is locked away or hidden from view)
  • in hotels

It is your responsibility to ensure a reasonable degree of protection for all ANZ Information assets in your possession.

3.6 Physical Security

All staff shall have security access cards to gain entry to building/floors. Do not let anyone tailgate. If you see anyone loitering or looking suspicious, report it to your manager.

3.7 E-mail

It is the responsibility of all employees to familiarise themselves with ANZ's e-mail policy and guidelines and to understand the use of e-mail encryption.

ANZ's e-mail system is to be used for legitimate ANZ business use only. It may therefore be monitored by ANZ to ensure policy compliance.

3.8 Internet

Internet access within ANZ premises must be accessed through the ANZ network. This is because you will have a degree of protection provided by the ANZ firewall.

Networked ANZ computers within ANZ premises must not be connected to a modem. Only stand-alone (non-networked) ANZ computers may be connected to a GIS approved dial-up access Internet account.

The Internet sites you visit through the ANZ firewall complex are for business purposes and therefore may be monitored, as is any information you download, to ensure compliance with ANZ policy.
