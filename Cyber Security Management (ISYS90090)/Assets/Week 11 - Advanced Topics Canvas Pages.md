## 11.0 Overview

[Canvas page](https://canvas.lms.unimelb.edu.au/courses/237535/pages/11-dot-0-overview?module_item_id=7679781)

The week extends the Horizon case, where the "crown jewels" moved from information to knowledge, particularly intellectual property (a category of knowledge asset). Section 11.2 explores knowledge and how it drives innovation, then presents a series of security controls for protecting knowledge assets.

Intended learning outcomes:

- Identify the differences between data, information and knowledge, and explain why they matter for securing organisational assets.
- Identify the generic flows between people, paper and the digital environment, and understand the risks from the gap between how the organisation protects these flows and how an external threat agent attacks them.
- Understand the range and types of security controls that can protect knowledge from leakage.

## 11.1 Knowledge leakage and intellectual property theft in organisations

[Canvas page](https://canvas.lms.unimelb.edu.au/courses/237535/pages/11-dot-1-knowledge-leakage-and-intellectual-property-theft-in-organisations?module_item_id=7679782)

- Controlling the circulation of information inside and across organisational boundaries is a critical modern security challenge.
- Sensitive information includes strategies, product information, unique industrial processes and techniques, and confidential client information.
- Short-term impacts of leakage: lost revenue, costs from breaching confidentiality agreements, productivity loss and effort to recover market position and client trust.
- Long-term impacts: loss of reputation and, most importantly, competitive erosion.
- Information exists in two states: **resident** (stored in a container) or **transmitted** (moving between containers).
- Pre-digital model (Figure 1): two containers (people and paper); information moves through conversation and the human acts of reading and writing.
- Digital model (Figure 2): a third container (digital media) adds flows such as printing, scanning, network transmission and photocopying. Information can also flow within the same container type, and can convert between digital and physical forms without human intervention (printing, scanning).
- Figures adapted from Ahmad & Ruighaver (2005), *An Information-Centric Approach to Data Security in Organizations* (IEEE Tencon 2005). The figure images were not captured here.
