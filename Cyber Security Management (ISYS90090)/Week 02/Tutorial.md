## Purpose

Use the CEO, CIO, and CISO role-play to examine who makes security decisions, what each leader needs from the others, and how security choices should support the business.

The reading's main point is that information security works when the three roles share business aims, risk information, and decision rights. Conflict grows when each role treats security through only its own goals (Fitzgerald, 2007).

## Tutorial Format

The lecturer uses "tutorial" and "workshop" interchangeably. Week 2 is the first two-hour workshop, and students should read Fitzgerald (2007) before attending.

1. Begin with questions and a review of the week's content.
2. Join the assigned CEO, CIO, or CISO group.
3. Define the role's goals, duties, information needs, and limits.
4. Prepare a position on security funding, acceptable risk, and ownership.
5. Ask the other two groups questions that expose gaps or competing aims.
6. Agree on a joint approach that supports the business and keeps risk within an accepted level.

## Role Reference

| Role | Main concern | Security responsibility | Needs from the other roles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CEO | Business direction, performance, reputation, and use of scarce funds | Set priorities, support sound security work, fund it, and hold leaders accountable | Clear risk, cost, legal, and business-impact advice |
| CIO | Business technology, delivery, availability, and efficient IT operations | Build security into systems and projects, maintain reliable services, and balance security with delivery | Business priorities from the CEO and workable risk advice from the CISO |
| CISO | Protection of organisational assets and management of security risk | Assess risk, propose treatment options, run security operations, and explain security in business terms | Risk appetite, priorities, resources, and clear ownership decisions from the CEO and CIO |

## Core Role Tensions

### Security Spending

- The CEO must weigh security against other uses of limited funds.
- The CIO needs controls that fit delivery plans and operating needs.
- The CISO must explain which risks justify spending and offer more than one cost-risk option.

The CISO advises and influences, but business leaders decide how much risk the organisation accepts.

### Security and Project Delivery

Security work added late can cause rework, delay, and resistance. The CIO and CISO should include security during early analysis and design, then scale the effort to the risk of the project.

### Availability and Business Continuity

The CIO must work with business leaders to identify critical services, business impact, and recovery needs. Security controls should protect confidentiality and integrity without causing avoidable loss of availability.

### Communication

The CISO needs broad security knowledge but must explain it through business effects, such as:

- Lost service or staff time.
- Legal and audit exposure.
- Harm to customers and reputation.
- Delayed products or projects.
- The cost of response and recovery.
- Business opportunities that safer systems can support.

Technical detail only helps when it supports the decision the audience must make.

### Security as Business Support

Information technology and security exist to support the organisation's mission. Security should not pursue maximum control without regard for cost or work needs. It should help the organisation operate, launch services, meet duties, and take informed risks.

## Role-Play Preparation

### If You Are the CEO

Prepare to discuss:

- The organisation's main goals and reputation risks.
- The minimum control level the business needs.
- Legal, audit, and customer duties.
- The value, cost, timing, and staffing needs of a proposed investment.
- Why this security action should take priority over other work.

Questions for the CISO and CIO:

- Which business risk does this proposal reduce?
- What evidence supports the stated likelihood and impact?
- What lower-cost or phased options exist?
- Who will own the result and report whether it works?

### If You Are the CIO

Prepare to discuss:

- Secure development and early security input.
- Service availability, recovery needs, and critical systems.
- Patch, configuration, access, testing, and audit work.
- The staff and systems needed to put controls into practice.
- How security work affects time, cost, and delivery.

Questions for the CISO and CEO:

- What risk level must the system meet?
- Which controls must form part of the project from the start?
- What business gain or audit issue will the investment address?
- How much security is enough for this system?

### If You Are the CISO

Prepare to discuss:

- The organisation's most important assets and risks.
- Cost-risk choices rather than one fixed proposal.
- New investment compared with the cost of ongoing operations.
- Useful security measures and reporting frequency.
- Business priorities, risk appetite, policy support, and incident ownership.

Questions for the CEO and CIO:

- Which business goals matter most over the next 12 to 18 months?
- Which security issues block or slow important work?
- What level of risk will the organisation accept?
- What should happen during a major incident, and who decides?

## Group Answer Structure

For each issue, prepare:

1. **Position:** What does your role want?
2. **Reason:** Which business, technology, or security need supports it?
3. **Trade-off:** What cost, delay, exposure, or lost opportunity might follow?
4. **Information gap:** What must another role explain?
5. **Joint decision:** What action could all three roles support?

## Discussion Questions

- Who should decide whether a cybersecurity risk is acceptable?
- How can the CISO show value when success often means that no incident occurs?
- When should the CIO reject or delay a security request?
- What information does the CEO need before approving security spending?
- How can poor reporting lines weaken security decisions?
- Which role owns the risk when a business unit uses technology outside central IT?

## Key Takeaways

- The CEO sets business direction and makes the final risk and funding decisions.
- The CIO joins business needs with secure and reliable technology delivery.
- The CISO informs and influences decisions through risk, control, and business-impact advice.
- Security should enter projects early and match the risk involved.
- Clear questions between the three roles reduce weak assumptions and unclear ownership.
- Shared business aims matter more than job titles.
